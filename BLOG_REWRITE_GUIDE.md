# Blog Rewrite Guide: angelphommachan.com

## Start of every session
Read this file and BLOG_LOG.md before doing anything. If BLOG_LOG.md doesn't exist, create it.

## Goal
Rewrite the existing blog posts on my site, one at a time. Each rewrite gets competitor research, a stronger headline, and only claims I can back up.

## Audience
Students and early-career marketers, mostly women, who follow communities like Sky Society. They want insights, trends, tools, and career moves they can use right away. They are smart and busy and have already read the generic version. Hiring managers will also read these posts, so they should show how I think.

## Personal and advice posts
- Share lived experience, not rules. Frame advice as "what worked for me" and say plainly that it may not fit everyone's situation.
- Never discourage or put down the reader or anyone else's path.
- Source material for Angel's story (her own writing, read before any personal post): ~/Downloads/scholarship essay.pdf, ~/Downloads/Finding Purpose_ My Journey to Growth and Community.pdf, ~/Downloads/Freshman Convocation Student Spotlight  .pdf, ~/Downloads/Why I Stayed.pdf.
- Sensitive details in those essays (mental health crisis and hospitalization, a parent's incarceration, special education) stay out of public posts unless Angel explicitly chooses to include them for that post.

## Rules against making things up
1. Every claim outside my own experience needs a linked external source. This includes stats, trends, platform changes, "studies show," and "most marketers."
2. Open every URL you cite with WebFetch and confirm it says what you claim. Never write a URL from memory.
3. Cite the original source, not a blog that repeated it. Name the source and year in the sentence. Prefer the last 12 months.
4. If you can't verify a claim, cut it and list it under Flags. Don't soften it into a vaguer version.
5. Don't invent quotes, people, survey results, or offers (freebies, templates, sign-ups) I don't have.
6. If a decision is missing, ask me. Don't guess.

## Sources
Prefer: official platform newsrooms and help docs (Google, Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn), Think with Google, HubSpot State of Marketing, Sprout Social Index, Pew Research.
Avoid: undated stats, stat roundup pages, paywalled numbers you can't read, and AI-generated listicles.

## Step 1: Inventory
List every blog post in the repo: file path, current title, one-line topic, word count, and whether it is evergreen or timely.
Flag any posts that cover the same topic and suggest whether to merge or split them.
Stop and let me pick the order.

## Step 2: Competitor research
For the post I pick:
- Search the topic and the questions a marketer would type about it. Review the top 5 to 8 results, including People Also Ask.
- Make a table: URL, publisher, headline, format (list, guide, opinion), what it misses or gets outdated.
- Write 2 to 3 sentences on the gap my post can fill.
- Competitor posts are research only. Don't copy their structure or wording, and don't link them in my post.
Stop and show me before writing headlines.

## Step 3: Headlines
Give me 5 options:
- 2 number-led (example pattern: "5 Things I Learned Running My First Google Ads Campaign")
- 2 question-led, using real questions from Step 2 (example pattern: "Is TikTok Still Worth It for Small Brands in 2026?")
- 1 wildcard

Rules:
- A number must match the real count of items in the post
- Under 60 characters, main topic in the first 50
- Timely posts include the year
- The post must deliver what the headline promises
- No "ultimate," "secrets," "game-changer," or "you won't believe"

Mark your pick with a one-line reason. Also give a meta description (under 155 characters). Keep the existing URL slug unless I approve a change; if it changes, flag that a redirect is needed.
Stop for my pick.

## Step 4: Rewrite
Match the flow and section style of [path to your best post].

Structure:
- Open with a 40 to 60 word direct answer to the headline. Don't restate the title.
- 3 to 5 key takeaways
- H2s phrased as reader questions where it fits
- One section from my own experience, using only the facts above
- At least one table or comparison where it helps
- Short FAQ (3 to 5) from People Also Ask
- End with one CTA pointing to [your contact page or portfolio case study URL]. Don't mention a newsletter until I add it here.

Links:
- 2 to 3 internal links to other posts or portfolio case studies on my site. Only link pages that exist in the repo.
- 2 to 3 external links to verified sources

Voice:
- First person for my experience, "you" for the reader
- Short, plain sentences. Active voice.
- No em dashes
- No filler or AI phrasing: leverage, utilize, unlock, dive in, landscape, game-changer, "in today's fast-paced world," "it's not X, it's Y," moreover, furthermore
- No hedging ("I feel like," "arguably")

Technical:
- Descriptive alt text for every image
- BlogPosting schema (JSON-LD) matching the final headline, meta description, slug, and dates. Keep the original publish date.
- Timely posts get a visible "Last updated" date
- Keep my site layout, fonts, and components. Only change post content and metadata.
