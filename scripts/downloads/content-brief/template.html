SEO CONTENT BRIEF TEMPLATE

SEO content brief

Plan one blog post before you write it. Fill it in order; most briefs take about 20 minutes.

Working titleWriterPublish date

01Reader and goal

ReaderWho is this for? One sentence, specific enough to picture.
GoalWhat should they know or be able to do when they finish?

02Search

Main keywordThe phrase your reader types into Google. Note how many people search it each month.
Related searchesTwo or three other phrases or questions people search (from "People also ask" and autocomplete).
What searchers wantSearch the keyword yourself. What kind of page shows up most: a how-to, a list, a review?

03Your angle

What's missingWhat none of the top three results do. Fill in page 2 first, then sum it up here.
What only you can addA story, photo, result, or example from your own experience.
Your angleThe one idea your post adds, in one sentence.
LengthAverage the top three, then divide by 3. It's a starting point, not a rule.

04Next step and proof

Next step for the readerThe one thing they should do after reading (your call to action). Decide it now.
How you'll know it workedOne number to check 30 days after publishing, like your Google ranking or clicks.
Ready to write? You should be able to say the reader, the goal, and what's missing in one sentence each. If you can't, keep researching before you draft.

Example: a filled-in brief

The brief behind “Owala vs. Stanley: New Colors or New Products?” on angelphommachan.com. Search numbers from Ahrefs, US.

ReaderPeople starting out in marketing who want to see how two brands grow after one hit product
GoalUnderstand the tradeoff between refreshing a best-seller and launching new products
Main keywordowala vs stanley (1,800 searches a month, easy to rank for)
Related searchesowala color drop (4,600), why is owala so popular (250), stanley marketing strategy
What searchers wantShoppers comparing the bottles: every top result is a product test
What's missingNo one compared the two brands’ marketing
What only I can addMy own side-by-side read of their products, ads, and blogs
AngleNew colors or new products: what each strategy costs and when it works
LengthAverage ÷ 3 ≈ 470 words; finished at about 1,480 to cover products, ads, and blogs
SourcesRetail Dive, Fast Company, The Drum, Owala’s Color Drop FAQ, ASI (each opened and checked)
Next step for the readerNotice where each brand’s ads and links send you, then answer: refresh it or build something new?
How I’ll know it workedGoogle ranking for “owala vs stanley” and time on page after 30 days
What changed after research: One quote I planned to use was no longer on Owala’s site, so it came out before drafting. A classmate pointed out that the main growth number came from a search tool companies use to order branded gifts, so the post now says that plainly.
