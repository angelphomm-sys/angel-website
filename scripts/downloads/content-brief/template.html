01Target audience and goal

Target audienceWho the post is for and what they already know. Specific enough to picture.e.g. Marketers starting out who write their own blog posts
Funnel stageWhere they are: just learning (awareness), comparing options (consideration), or ready to act (decision).e.g. Awareness☐ Awareness ☐ Consideration ☐ Decision
Content goalWhat the post should do for the reader, and for you (traffic, trust, sign-ups, sales).e.g. Readers can write a brief; I earn template downloads

02Keyword research

Free tools work: the Ahrefs Keyword Generator, Google's autocomplete, and the "People also ask" box.

Primary keywordThe main phrase you want to rank for. Add monthly search volume and keyword difficulty (KD) if your tool shows them.e.g. seo content brief (500 a month, KD 1)
Secondary keywordsTwo or three related phrases the post should also cover.e.g. seo content brief template, content brief template
Search intentWhy people search the primary keyword. Search it yourself and see what kind of page ranks.e.g. Informational: step-by-step guides, most with a template☐ Informational (learn) ☐ Commercial (compare) ☐ Transactional (buy)
Questions to answerQuestions from "People also ask" and autocomplete. These become headings and FAQs.e.g. What is an SEO content brief? What should it include?

04Content angle

Content gapWhat none of the top results do. Sum up section 03.e.g. Every guide is written for agency teams, not people writing their own posts
Unique valueFirst-hand experience only you can add: a story, photo, result, or example from your own work.e.g. My own filled-in brief and what I'd change
Editorial angleThe one idea your post adds, in one sentence.e.g. A plain-language brief for people who write their own posts
Target word countA range. Average the top three and divide by 3 for a starting point, not a rule.e.g. 900 to 1,200 words

05On-page SEO

What people see in Google before they click, and the address of the page.

SEO title (title tag)Primary keyword near the start. About 50 to 60 characters so Google doesn't cut it off.e.g. How to Write an SEO Content Brief (With a Free Template)
Meta descriptionThe summary under your title in Google. About 150 to 160 characters. Google may rewrite it.e.g. How to write an SEO content brief in seven plain steps, what has changed in search and what hasn't, plus a free template you can download.
URL slugThe end of the web address. Short, lowercase, words separated by hyphens.e.g. /blog/how-to-write-an-seo-content-brief
Ready to write? You should be able to name the target audience, the search intent, and the content gap in one sentence each. If you can't, keep researching before you draft.

08Call to action and KPIs

Call to action (CTA)The one thing the reader should do after reading, plus the link.e.g. Download the template
KPI (success metric)One number that shows the post worked. Check it 30 days after publishing.e.g. Template downloads and Google ranking for the primary keyword

09Brand voice and guidelines

Most useful when someone else is writing. If you write it yourself, use it to remember the rules you set.

Brand voice and toneHow the post should sound.e.g. Plain and friendly, first person
Must includeRequired lines, disclaimers, product names, or terms.e.g. A credit line for every image
AvoidClaims, words, or topics to leave out.e.g. Any number I can't link to a source

10Pre-publish checklist

Primary keyword in the SEO title, the first 100 words, and one H2
SEO title and meta description match section 05
Every number links to a source you opened
Two or three internal links with clear anchor text
Images credited, each with alt text (a short description for screen readers)
The call to action matches the content goal in section 01
A calendar reminder to check your KPI in 30 days

The brief behind “Owala vs. Stanley: New Colors or New Products?” on angelphommachan.com. Search numbers from Ahrefs, US. Section word counts are from the finished post.
