Ghia's marketing strategy sells the hour before dinner to people who still drink. Founder Melanie Masarin told Hannah's List in August 2026 that about 92 percent of Ghia's buyers identify as alcohol consumers, so the non-alcoholic aperitif never asks anyone to quit. The smartest money in that plan went to restaurants that barely paid it back.

**The short version**

- Ghia markets to drinkers who want a night off, because that is who buys it.
- It sells an occasion, the 6 p.m. ritual, along with the glass and the cookbook that go with it, which is how a $38 bottle makes sense.
- In 2022 its restaurant work took up to 40 percent of marketing resources for about 4 percent of revenue, and that trade is the part worth copying.

## Who is Ghia's target customer?

It's tempting to file Ghia under sober-curious, the shelf of drinks for people cutting back or quitting. Masarin's own numbers put it somewhere else. In the [Hannah's List interview](https://hannahslist.substack.com/p/q-and-a-melanie-masarin-of-ghia) from August 2026, she described her buyers as people who want "great wine on a Saturday and something non-alcoholic on a Tuesday," and she said plainly that Ghia is "not a cessation product." "Positioning Ghia against alcohol would have alienated most of the people we made it for."

She expects that habit to grow. Masarin told [The Daily Pour](https://thedailypour.com/no-low-alcohol/melanie-masarin-ghia-interview/) in March 2026 that "people will alternate more between alcoholic and non-alcoholic options," which means Ghia's growth sits with drinkers who switch on some nights. A brand built for that customer has no reason to talk about streaks or willpower, so Ghia talks about a good time.

Ghia's Instagram post from January 22, 2026, shows the approach in practice. Notice what the caption leaves out: in the month built around Dry January, it simply waves at anyone "new here."

The lesson reaches past drinks. If your product gets filed under a niche, check who actually buys it before you choose an enemy, because the enemy you pick decides who feels welcome.

## Ghia sells the 6 p.m. ritual, and its shop proves it

Because its buyers already drink, Ghia doesn't have to sell a replacement for alcohol. It sells the moment alcohol usually fills. Masarin told [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ghia-emerging-category) in 2023 that she built Ghia for specific occasions, like "the drinks at the bar before dinner" and a friend's dinner party. By 2026, she was calling it "the 6pm ritual" in the Hannah's List interview.

So Ghia designed the ritual itself. Shopify reported that the company created its own glass, a stemless martini glass with a totem foot, and that it became one of the brand's big sellers. Ghia's site now sells Totem glasses, an ice bucket, cocktail boxes, and Riviera, the cookbook Masarin published in April 2026 from her grandmother's recipes in the South of France.

An occasion gives a brand more to sell and more to post about than a category does. Every one of those products belongs at 6 p.m., and none of them has to mention alcohol.

## Why is Ghia so expensive?

The ritual also carries the price. A bottle of Ghia cost $38 in 2023, and Masarin told Shopify the brand got pushback because people felt a drink without alcohol should not cost the same as one with it. Her answer was the ingredients. Without alcohol to preserve them, she estimated that what goes into a bottle of Ghia costs six to 10 times more than the ingredients in a comparable alcoholic drink.

Ghia answered the pushback with customer education and kept the price, which fits what Masarin told Modern Retail in 2022: she never wanted "a lesser version of an alcoholic brand," and a cheaper bottle would have said exactly that. A premium holds when a buyer can repeat the reason in one sentence, and Ghia's reason (natural ingredients cost more without alcohol to keep them stable) fits in one.

## Why did Ghia spend on restaurants that barely paid?

Ghia was supposed to start in restaurants. Masarin told Hannah's List the plan was to launch on April 1, 2020, in about 50 restaurants only, and every restaurant closed two weeks before, so Ghia launched online. By January 2022, she told [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/unapologetically-loud-and-fun-ghias-melanie-masarin-on-the-sober-curious-movement/), 75 percent of the business was still online and Ghia was on more than 100 menus in America. Even so, Ghia was spending about 30 to 40 percent of its marketing resources on chefs, sampling, and tastings. That work brought in about 4 percent of revenue, and she called it "very much a long-term play."

<Bars caption="Ghia's restaurant channel, January 2022" source="Modern Retail interview with Melanie Masarin, January 2022." items={[{label: "Marketing resources, top of the 30 to 40 percent range", value: 40, display: "up to 40%", highlight: true}, {label: "Share of revenue", value: 4, display: "about 4%"}]} />

Four years later she still defends the trade. She told Hannah's List that getting into bars and restaurants "isn't the highest-ROI thing we do" from a pure sales and volume view, but those rooms are where Ghia builds affinity with customers and gets discovered. Her reasoning goes back to the customer: if people can only enjoy a non-alcoholic night at home on the couch, the brand hasn't finished its job. She still DMs restaurants and brings samples when she eats out.

The menu is where the positioning becomes visible. Masarin described the goal as a drink you can pour at aperitivo hour "next to someone's Negroni without anyone blinking," and a restaurant is the one place a drinker sees that scene for real. The spend that looks wasteful on a revenue chart is the channel doing the positioning work.

## What happened to Ghia after Shark Tank?

Masarin pitched Ghia in Season 14 of Shark Tank and asked for $250,000 for 5 percent. Kevin O'Leary countered at $250,000 for 10 percent, and she walked away without a deal, according to a July 2026 [Takeout recap](https://www.thetakeout.com/1872877/what-happened-shark-tank-ghia/). After the show, Ghia went into Whole Foods and Wegmans and later Target, The Takeout reported.

Ghia's own post from September 22, 2026, asks, "Who says you can't color hunt at Whole Foods?" Even the grocery post sells color and mood, and it never mentions quitting.

<Instagram url="https://www.instagram.com/p/DdmMgSAlbRh/" title="Ghia at Whole Foods, September 2026" />

Ghia is still in business, and it is growing more carefully. Masarin told Hannah's List that two years ago the company decided to take the business to profitability, which meant downsizing the team and being more selective about growth. "Most of my job now is saying no to protect the business," she said. Saying no still leaves room for restaurants, because she told The Daily Pour she thinks a lot about "where and how people meet the brand."

## Where I'd push Ghia: give restaurants their own number

The easy read on Ghia is a pretty bottle that rode the sober-curious wave. I think that misses the strategy. Ghia grew by selling a ritual to drinkers and by paying for restaurant seats that don't pay back on paper, and I'd keep both. What I'd change is how the restaurant channel gets measured, because a channel defended on affinity and discovery needs a number that tracks them.

Athletic Brewing shows one way to get it. For its 2026 Athletic January, the brand added an OpenTable map of bars and restaurants serving its beer and a $5 rebate on one can or draft pour, [Beverage Industry reported](https://www.bevindustry.com/articles/98051-athletic-brewing-co-relaunches-athletic-january-campaign) in December 2025. A redeemed rebate is a trial you can count. For Ghia, I would track the share of new customers who buy again within 90 days, split by whether they first met the brand in a restaurant or on a shelf. If the restaurant group comes back more often, that 4 percent of revenue starts to look cheap.

## What to steal from Ghia's marketing strategy

1. **Market to the buyers you have.** Check who buys before you pick an enemy.
2. **Sell the occasion, then sell what fills it.** Ghia named the 6 p.m. ritual and built a glass, an ice bucket, and a cookbook around it.
3. **Give the price a reason people can repeat.** Ghia points to ingredients that cost six to 10 times more.
4. **Fund one channel for memory, and give it its own number.** Measure repeat purchases by where a customer first met you.

Graza had the same instinct in [olive oil](/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza), and Fishwife had it in [tinned fish](/blog/fishwife-marketing-strategy). For another drinks comparison, see how [Liquid Death and Poppi](/blog/liquid-death-vs-poppi-marketing) built their brands.

Before your next launch, write down two things: the hour your product belongs to, and the first place a stranger will see someone else enjoying it. If the only answer to the second is your own feed, that is the channel to fund next.
