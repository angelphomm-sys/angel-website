About 92 percent of the people who buy Ghia drink alcohol. That number explains why the non-alcoholic aperitif never asks anyone to quit. Ghia's marketing strategy sells an occasion, the hour before dinner, instead of abstinence, and its most expensive bet, restaurants, brings in about 4 percent of revenue on purpose.

**The short version**

- **Ghia positions around an occasion, not a category.** It markets to drinkers who want a night off, which is most of its buyers.
- **It sells the ritual and everything that fills it:** the 6 p.m. pour, the glass, the ice bucket, and the cookbook.
- **The $38 price holds because the reason fits in one sentence:** the ingredients cost more when there is no alcohol to preserve them.
- **Restaurants took up to 40 percent of marketing resources in 2022 for about 4 percent of revenue.** It's the smartest part of the plan and the part that most needs a number.

## What gap did Ghia's founder see?

Mélanie Masarin grew up spending summers near the Mediterranean, and on Ghia's [Our Story page](https://drinkghia.com/pages/our-story) she writes about her mother and grandmother making giant jugs of limoncello from freshly picked lemons. What she loved most about those nights was being together, letting loose, and laughing with friends, and she wanted a drink that could take people there "without numbing the night," one "you'd remember in the morning." Her stated goal is to "take back the word drinking from alcohol."

The gap was an hour with nothing good to pour. Ghia was built for specific moments, [Shopify wrote](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ghia-emerging-category) in 2023, like "the drinks at the bar before dinner" and a friend's dinner party. Alcohol owns those hours, so the product had to look and taste like it belonged at the bar.

Starting from a scene instead of a health claim gave Ghia a filter for every later decision: does this belong at 6 p.m.?

## Who is Ghia's target customer?

Ghia sits on the shelf next to drinks for people cutting back or quitting, but its buyers don't fit that label. In an August 2026 [interview with Hannah's List](https://hannahslist.substack.com/p/q-and-a-melanie-masarin-of-ghia), Masarin said about 92 percent of Ghia's buyers identify as alcohol consumers. She described them as people who want "great wine on a Saturday and something non-alcoholic on a Tuesday," and called Ghia "not a cessation product." Her reasoning: "Positioning Ghia against alcohol would have alienated most of the people we made it for."

She expects that habit to spread. "People will alternate more between alcoholic and non-alcoholic options," she told [The Daily Pour](https://thedailypour.com/no-low-alcohol/melanie-masarin-ghia-interview/) in March 2026, which puts Ghia's growth with drinkers who switch on some nights. A brand built for that customer has no reason to talk about streaks or willpower, so Ghia talks about a good time. Even in January, the month built around quitting, its Instagram post from January 22, 2026, simply waves at anyone "new here."

<Instagram url="https://www.instagram.com/p/DT0pxDyFRFx/" title="Ghia on Instagram, January 2026" />

Before you pick an enemy for your brand, check who actually buys from you. Ghia's real customers would have heard an anti-alcohol message as a message about someone else.

## Why is Ghia so expensive?

A bottle of the Original Apéritif cost $38 in 2023 and still lists at $38 on Ghia's site. Masarin told Shopify the brand got pushback from people who felt a drink without alcohol shouldn't cost the same as one with it. Her answer was the ingredients: without alcohol to preserve them, she estimated that what goes into a bottle of Ghia costs six to 10 times more than the ingredients in a comparable alcoholic drink.

<Figure src="/images/blog/behind-the-strategy-ghia/ghia-original-aperitif-ingredients.jpg" alt="A Ghia Original Apéritif bottle embossed with the words clear-eyed, beside a list of ingredients: white grape, yuzu, lemon balm, elderflower, gentian root, plum, rhubarb root, ginger, and rosemary" caption="Ghia puts the ingredient list next to the bottle on its product page, which turns the price argument into something you can read. Image: Ghia." ratio="1/1" />

Ghia kept the price and explained it. That matches what Masarin told [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/unapologetically-loud-and-fun-ghias-melanie-masarin-on-the-sober-curious-movement/) in January 2022, that she never wanted "a lesser version of an alcoholic brand." A cheaper bottle would have said exactly that. A premium holds when a buyer can repeat the reason in one sentence, and Ghia's reason fits in one.

## Ghia sells the 6 p.m. ritual, and its shop proves it

Because its buyers already drink, Ghia doesn't have to replace alcohol. It sells the moment alcohol usually fills, which Masarin was calling "the 6pm ritual" by 2026, and then it sells the objects for that moment. Shopify reported in 2023 that Ghia designed its own glass, a stemless martini glass with a totem foot, and that it became one of the brand's best sellers. The site now lists Totem Glasses as a handblown set of two designed with Sophie Lou Jacobsen, for $110.

<Figure src="/images/blog/behind-the-strategy-ghia/ghia-totem-glasses-palma.jpg" alt="Two handblown Ghia Totem glasses filled with a red drink and ice, one with a green bulb foot and one with an amber bulb and green base" caption="The Totem glass gives the ritual a signature object, and every colorway makes a new photo. Image: Ghia." ratio="1/1" />

The rest of the shop extends the same occasion: an ice bucket, a cocktail box with a bottle, a pour spout, and a Totem glass, and the Riviera Kitchen Set, which pairs the aperitif with Riviera, the cookbook Masarin published in 2026. Ghia's product page says the book collects 101 recipes from the coast of France and Italy, guided by her grandmother's kitchen rules.

<Figure src="/images/blog/behind-the-strategy-ghia/ghia-riviera-cookbook-kitchen-set.jpg" alt="A bottle of Ghia Original Apéritif standing beside the Riviera cookbook by Mélanie Masarin, its cream cover showing a fish on a platter" caption="The kitchen set sells dinner along with the drink, which moves Ghia from the bar cart to the table. Image: Ghia." ratio="1/1" />

The copy keeps the tone light. The newsletter sign-up reads "We're booze free, but our emails are intoxicating," and the bottle's shoulder is embossed with "clear-eyed." Both lines admit there's no alcohol and move straight to the fun. Owning an occasion gives a brand more to sell and more to post about than owning a category, and none of these products has to mention what's missing.

## Why did Ghia spend on restaurants that barely paid?

Ghia was meant to launch in restaurants. Masarin told Hannah's List the plan was about 50 restaurants on April 1, 2020, and every one of them closed two weeks before, so Ghia launched online instead. By January 2022, she told Modern Retail, 75 percent of the business was still online and Ghia was on more than 100 menus in America. Even so, it spent about 30 to 40 percent of its marketing resources on chefs, sampling, and tastings for about 4 percent of revenue. She called it "very much a long-term play."

<Bars caption="Ghia's restaurant channel, January 2022" source="Modern Retail interview with Melanie Masarin, January 2022." items={[{label: "Marketing resources, top of the 30 to 40 percent range", value: 40, display: "up to 40%", highlight: true}, {label: "Share of revenue", value: 4, display: "about 4%"}]} />

Four years later she still defends it. Bars and restaurants "isn't the highest-ROI thing we do" on sales and volume alone, she told Hannah's List, but those rooms are where Ghia builds affinity and gets discovered. If people can only enjoy a non-alcoholic night on their own couch, she argued, the brand hasn't finished its job. She still DMs restaurants and brings samples when she eats out.

The restaurant is where the positioning becomes real. Masarin's goal is a drink you can pour at aperitivo hour "next to someone's Negroni without anyone blinking," and a bar is the only place a drinker sees that scene happen.

<Figure src="/images/blog/behind-the-strategy-ghia/ghia-original-aperitif-vs-negroni.jpg" alt="Ghia graphic comparing the Original Apéritif with a Negroni: 0 percent versus 24 percent alcohol by volume, 3 grams versus 10 grams of sugar, and 20 versus 190 calories" caption="Ghia's own side-by-side sets the aperitif next to a Negroni, the same pairing Masarin wants to see on a bar. Image: Ghia." ratio="1/1" />

A shelf tells people Ghia exists. A bar shows them a stranger ordering it, and that is a kind of proof a revenue chart can't measure.

## What happened to Ghia after Shark Tank?

Masarin pitched Ghia in Season 14 of Shark Tank, asking for $250,000 for 5 percent. Kevin O'Leary countered at $250,000 for 10 percent, and she walked away without a deal, according to [The Takeout's recap](https://www.thetakeout.com/1872877/what-happened-shark-tank-ghia/), last updated in July 2026. Ghia then went into Whole Foods and Wegmans, and later Target.

Its September 22, 2026, Instagram post from Whole Foods asks who says you can't "color hunt" there. Even the grocery post sells color and mood.

<Instagram url="https://www.instagram.com/p/DdmMgSAlbRh/" title="Ghia at Whole Foods, September 2026" />

Retail also changed what Ghia sells. The site now carries Le Spritz, canned spritzes in flavors such as Lime & Salt, Ghia-Ginger, and Sumac & Chili, and the ice bucket is described as fitting ten Ghia cans. A can is easier to grab off a grocery shelf than a $38 bottle, and the colors keep it recognizable from across an aisle.

<Figure src="/images/blog/behind-the-strategy-ghia/ghia-le-spritz-cans.jpg" alt="Five Ghia Le Spritz cans stacked on a black background, in green, blue, red, burgundy, and pale yellow, each with the Ghia script logo" caption="Each Le Spritz flavor gets its own color but keeps the same slanted Ghia script, so the cans read as one family on a shelf. Image: Ghia." ratio="1/1" />

Ghia is growing more carefully now. Masarin told Hannah's List that two years ago the company decided to take the business to profitability, which meant downsizing the team and being more selective. "Most of my job now is saying no to protect the business," she said. Restaurants survived those cuts, because, as she told The Daily Pour, she thinks a lot about "where and how people meet the brand."

## What to take from Ghia's marketing strategy

1. **Market to the buyers you have.** Most of Ghia's buyers drink, so it never made alcohol the enemy.
2. **Start from a scene you can describe.** A table at 6 p.m. gives every new product a test to pass.
3. **Own the occasion, then sell what fills it.** Ghia named the ritual and built a glass, an ice bucket, and a cookbook kit around it.
4. **Give the price a reason people can repeat.** Ingredients that cost six to 10 times more, listed on the product page.
5. **Fund one channel for memory, and measure it.** Restaurants show a stranger ordering your drink, which a shelf can't.

Graza ran a similar playbook in [olive oil](/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza), and so did Fishwife in [tinned fish](/blog/fishwife-marketing-strategy). For a drinks brand that went the opposite way on health claims, see [Liquid Death vs. Poppi](/blog/liquid-death-vs-poppi-marketing).

## The catch: the restaurant bet still needs a number

Ghia is often read as a pretty bottle that rode the sober-curious wave. The bigger risk is the opposite reading: that every brand should copy its restaurant spend because a smart founder defends it. I'd keep the spend and change how it's measured. Masarin's case rests on affinity and discovery, and neither shows up on a revenue chart where restaurants earned about 4 percent.

That gap matters more now. A company focused on profitability will eventually defend every line of its budget, and a channel justified by feel is the easiest one to cut. Athletic Brewing shows a way to put a number on it. For its 2026 Athletic January, the brand published an OpenTable map of bars and restaurants serving its beer and offered a $5 rebate on one can or draft pour, [Beverage Industry reported](https://www.bevindustry.com/articles/98051-athletic-brewing-co-relaunches-athletic-january-campaign) in December 2025. Every redeemed rebate is a trial you can count.

For Ghia, I'd track the share of new customers who buy again within 90 days, split by whether they first met the brand in a restaurant or on a shelf. If the restaurant group comes back more often, that 4 percent of revenue starts to look cheap.

Before your next launch, write down two things: the hour your product belongs to, and the first place a stranger will see someone else enjoying it. If the only answer to the second is your own feed, that's the channel to fund next, and the first one to measure.
