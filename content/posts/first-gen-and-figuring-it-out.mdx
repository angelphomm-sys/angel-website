What did you think your life would look like after high school?

I had an answer, because a class made me put it in a slideshow. The assignment asked about our dreams after graduation: where we were from, what opportunities we thought we had, what we wanted to be, who inspired us, and where we would be in five years. Mine listed two careers, esthetician and photographer, with a few creators I followed online and Angelina Jolie as my inspirations.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/highschool-dream-slideshow.png" alt="A slideshow title card reading Dreams Don't Work Unless You Do in brush lettering over a field of purple wildflowers and mountains" caption="The title slide of my high school dream slideshow." ratio="16/9" />

It's a little silly looking back. But I meant every part of it, and here's what surprised me: I did it.

## Reaching the plan from my high school slideshow

I was born in Tecumseh, Nebraska, and grew up in Warroad, Minnesota, a small town five miles from the Canadian border, in an immigrant family without much formal education. You can only picture the kind of life you've seen, and that slideshow was the best future I could imagine.

After graduation, I went to work at the window factory in town, where most of my family worked too. I went in when it was dark and came out when it was dark. One day a coworker told me, "Get out of here before it's too late."

<Figure src="/images/me/story/marvin-window-frame.jpg" alt="A bare wooden window frame held in a large machine on a factory floor, with stacks of lumber behind it" caption="A window frame on the line at the factory where I worked after high school." ratio="4/5" />

So I left. I moved to Utah to start over, trained in esthetics, and worked as an esthetician. I picked up a camera and built a portrait photography business. Both careers from the slideshow came true.

Still, something was missing. I was working hard, but I was only choosing from the paths I had been shown, and I didn't know what else was out there.

## Becoming a first-generation college student

My boyfriend suggested college as a way to figure out my next step, so I enrolled at Utah Valley University (UVU). He couldn't help much with the process, because he had never had to navigate it on his own. I worked through the application, the FAFSA, and all the small things nobody tells you when you're the first in your family to go to college.

That's what being a [first-generation college student](https://www.firstgenforward.org/our-opportunity/are-you-a-first-gen-student) meant for me: learning the system while I was already inside it.

Choosing UVU wasn't really my decision. Staying was.

## Why I stayed at Utah Valley University

When I started, part of me still held on to the old plan. Changing direction felt like failing, as if the plan were a promise and walking away from it made me unreliable. College gave me the freedom to see it differently. I don't mean the freedom to do whatever I want, or the kind that comes with a bigger paycheck. I mean the freedom to learn who I am, see what options I have, and choose my own path.

That freedom came from rooms I had never been in. I joined the first group of Hope Scholars, a new scholarship program at UVU's Woodbury School of Business, and spoke at donor events and board meetings about what the support meant to me. Those rooms opened doors. I met community and business leaders, took on leadership roles, and worked [marketing internships](/work) across different industries.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/hope-scholars-event.jpg" alt="Angel Phommachan taking a selfie with six fellow Hope Scholars wearing name tags at a program event" caption="With fellow Hope Scholars at a program event." ratio="4/5" />

I earned my associate degree with honors, and I'm on track to finish my bachelor's degree in digital marketing in May 2027. I studied abroad in Italy, met people living lives I couldn't have described in high school, and I'm now preparing for graduate school.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/rome-colosseum-uvu.jpg" alt="A UVU study abroad group holding a Utah Valley Wolverines flag in front of the Colosseum in Rome" caption="My UVU study abroad group at the Colosseum in Rome." ratio="4/5" />

The writer and teacher bell hooks called education "the practice of freedom." Before college, freedom was a word I understood. Now I know what it looks like.

## Changing your plan isn't failing

Five years after that slideshow, I'm not an esthetician anymore. I'm still a photographer and a creative, and I'm about to become the first person in my family to finish a bachelor's degree. I didn't fail the plan. I finished it, and then I outgrew it.

Outgrowing something isn't the same as contradicting yourself. Contradicting yourself is pretending you never said it. Evolving is being able to say, "I believed that once, and I don't anymore." People don't always leave room for that. They get attached to the version of you they already understand, and when you change, they say "you changed" like an accusation. Sometimes the person holding on to the old version is you.

The old plan wasn't a mistake. It gave me something to work toward when I had nothing else to aim at. But nothing about it was permanent, and there was never a deadline for what came next. If you're further from your old plan than you expected, you didn't betray the person who made it. You just stopped treating her plan like a life sentence.

## What being the first in my family means to me

When I look at that slideshow now, I notice what's missing. There's no slide about earning a degree. No one in my family had done it, so it never occurred to me to put it on the list.

Being the first means no one walked this path ahead of me. It also means I get to show my younger siblings that the path exists. I spent years putting my family first as the oldest child, and now the best thing I can give them is proof.

What comes next is graduate school and a career I get to choose. I don't know every step yet, and for the first time, that doesn't scare me. I have a voice now, and I plan to use it for the people who come after me.
