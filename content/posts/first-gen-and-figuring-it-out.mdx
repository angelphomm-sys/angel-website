"Why did you choose UVU?"

People have asked me that since my first semester, and the honest answer is that I didn't. Someone suggested it, and I said yes.

Staying was my choice. It took me a while to understand why, and the answer goes back to a slideshow I made in high school.

## The future I could picture in high school

I grew up in Warroad, Minnesota, a small town five miles from the Canadian border, in an immigrant family without much formal education. In high school, I made a slideshow about my future. It had my hometown, a career as an esthetician, the people I looked up to, and where I would be in five years.

You can only picture the kind of life you've seen. That slideshow was the best future I could imagine, and I meant every part of it.

## When the plan ran out

After graduation, I went to work at the window factory in town, where most of my family worked too. I went in when it was dark and came out when it was dark. One day a coworker told me, "Get out of here before it's too late."

So I left. I moved to Utah to start over, trained and worked as an esthetician for a while, and picked up side jobs to cover the rest. I was working hard, but nothing felt like it was mine. I only knew the paths I had been shown.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/marvin-window-frame.jpg" alt="A bare wooden window frame held in a large machine on a factory floor, with stacks of lumber behind it" caption="A window frame on the line at the factory where I worked after high school." ratio="4/5" />

## Becoming a first-generation college student

My boyfriend suggested college as a way to figure out my next step, so I tried it. He couldn't help much with the process, because he had never had to navigate it on his own. I worked through the application, the FAFSA, and all the small things nobody tells you when you're the first in your family to go to college.

That's what being a [first-generation college student](https://www.firstgenforward.org/our-opportunity/are-you-a-first-gen-student) meant for me: learning the system while I was already inside it.

Choosing UVU wasn't my decision. Staying was, and I stayed because of freedom.

## Why I stayed at UVU

When I started, part of me still held on to the slideshow. Changing direction felt like failing, as if the plan were a promise and walking away from it made me unreliable. UVU gave me the freedom to see it differently. I don't mean the freedom to do whatever I want, or the kind that comes with a bigger paycheck. I mean the freedom to learn who I am, see what options I have, and choose my own path.

That freedom came from rooms I had never been in. As one of the first Hope Scholars at UVU's Woodbury School of Business, I spoke at donor events and board meetings and shared my story with people who invest in students like me. Those rooms opened doors. I met community and business leaders, took on leadership roles, and worked [marketing internships](/work) across different industries.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/hope-scholars-event.jpg" alt="Angel Phommachan taking a selfie with six fellow Hope Scholars wearing name tags at a program event" caption="With fellow Hope Scholars at a program event." ratio="4/5" />

I earned my associate degree with honors, and I'm on track to finish my bachelor's in digital marketing in May 2027. I studied abroad in Italy and met people living lives I couldn't have described in high school.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/rome-colosseum-uvu.jpg" alt="A UVU study abroad group holding a Utah Valley Wolverines flag in front of the Colosseum in Rome" caption="My UVU study abroad group at the Colosseum in Rome." ratio="4/5" />

bell hooks calls education "the practice of freedom." Before UVU, freedom was a word I understood. Now I know what it looks like.

## The plan didn't fall apart. I outgrew it.

Outgrowing something isn't the same as contradicting yourself. Contradicting yourself is pretending you never said it. Evolving is being able to say, "I believed that once, and I don't anymore." People don't always leave room for that. They get attached to the version of you they already understand, and when you change, they say "you changed" like an accusation. Sometimes the person holding on to the old version is you.

That doesn't make the old plan a mistake. It gave me something to work toward when I had nothing else to aim at. It was just early. If you're further from your old plan than you expected, you didn't betray the person who made it. You just stopped treating her plan like a life sentence.

## What the slideshow got right

Not long ago, I thought about that slideshow again and looked past the details. The hometown, the career, and the five-year timeline are all gone. One thing is still there: I wanted to help people.

Part of my work now is helping tell the Hope Scholars story to the people who fund it, so the next first-gen student doesn't have to figure everything out alone. The career changed, and so did the timeline. The reason didn't.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/childhood-doorway.jpg" alt="A toddler in a lavender shirt and pink pants standing in a bedroom doorway, holding the door frame and smiling" caption="Me, long before any plan." ratio="4/5" />

I left home for my dreams and built a second home here. I didn't choose UVU. But staying is how I found my way back to the plan I thought I had left behind.
