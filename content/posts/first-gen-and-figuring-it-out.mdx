What did you think your life would look like after high school?

In high school, a class asked us to map out our dreams after graduation in a slideshow: where we came from, the opportunities we saw, what we wanted to be, who inspired us, and where we would be in five years. Mine listed two careers, esthetician and photographer, and the YouTube creators I looked up to. I also wanted 10 corgis.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/highschool-dream-slideshow.png" alt="A slideshow title card reading Dreams Don't Work Unless You Do in brush lettering over a field of purple wildflowers and mountains" ratio="16/9" />

It sounds a little silly now, but I meant every part of it.

## Reaching the plan from my high school slideshow

I was born in Tecumseh, Nebraska, and grew up in Warroad, Minnesota, a small town five miles from the Canadian border, in an immigrant family without much formal education. After graduation, I worked at the window factory in town, where most of my family worked too. I knew I wanted more than the paths I had seen, so I moved to Utah to start over.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/marvin-window-frame.jpg" alt="A bare wooden window frame held in a large machine on a factory floor, with stacks of lumber behind it" ratio="4/5" />

I kept up photography after high school, and I've been a portrait photographer since 2018. In 2021, I graduated from the esthiology program at Aveda Institute Provo as valedictorian, then worked in the industry for a couple of years. Both careers from my slideshow came true.

Over time, though, the passion for esthetics faded, and I started wondering what else was possible.

## Becoming a first-generation college student

My boyfriend suggested college as a way to find my next step, so I enrolled at Utah Valley University (UVU). He couldn't help much with the process, because he had never had to navigate it on his own, so I learned the application, the FAFSA, and all the small things nobody tells you when you're the first in your family to go to college.

Choosing UVU wasn't really my decision. Staying was.

## Why I stayed at Utah Valley University

College gave me a kind of freedom I hadn't had before: the freedom to learn who I am, see what options I have, and choose my own path.

That freedom came from rooms I had never been in. I joined the first group of Hope Scholars, a new scholarship program at UVU's Woodbury School of Business, and spoke at donor events and board meetings about what that support meant to me. I met community and business leaders, took on leadership roles, and worked [marketing internships](/work) across different industries.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/hope-scholars-event.jpg" alt="Angel Phommachan taking a selfie with six fellow Hope Scholars wearing name tags at a program event" ratio="4/5" />

I earned my associate degree with honors, studied abroad in Italy, and I'm on track to finish my bachelor's degree in digital marketing in May 2027.

<Figure src="/images/me/story/rome-colosseum-uvu.jpg" alt="A UVU study abroad group holding a Utah Valley Wolverines flag in front of the Colosseum in Rome" ratio="4/5" />

## Changing your plan isn't failing

Five years after that slideshow, I'm not an esthetician anymore. I'm still a photographer and a creative, and I'm preparing for graduate school. I didn't fail the plan. I finished it, and then I outgrew it.

At first, changing direction felt like breaking a promise. Now I see the difference between contradicting yourself and evolving: evolving is being able to say, "I believed that once, and I don't anymore." The old plan gave me something to aim for. It was never meant to be permanent, and there was no deadline for what came next.

## What being the first in my family means to me

When I look at that slideshow now, I notice what's missing. There's no slide about earning a degree. No one in my family had done it, so it never occurred to me to dream about it.

My parents built a life in this country after leaving Laos and Thailand. Being the first in my family to earn a degree is how I carry that forward. It also means my younger siblings will grow up knowing this path exists, because they watched someone they love walk it.

In *As a Man Thinketh*, James Allen wrote, "Dream lofty dreams, and as you dream, so shall you become." For most of my life, my dreams were only as big as what I had seen. Education changed what I can see. The writer and teacher bell hooks called it "the practice of freedom," and that's exactly what it became for me.

If my story shows anything, it's that where you come from doesn't have to decide where you end up. I grew up thinking my options were the jobs I could see from my hometown. Now I have the privilege of choosing what I want to do with my life, and a voice I can use to help the people coming up behind me do the same. I don't take either one for granted.

In May 2027, I'll finish my degree. Then comes graduate school and a career I get to choose. I don't know every step yet, and for the first time, that excites me more than it scares me.

Little Angel made the best plan she could with the life she had seen. I get to make mine with everything I haven't seen yet.
