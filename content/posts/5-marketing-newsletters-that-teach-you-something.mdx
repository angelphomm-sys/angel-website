You do not need more marketing newsletters. The best marketing newsletters teach one mechanic you can reuse, not just the week's news. These five cover brand decisions, social teams, food trends, live events, and buyer psychology. Two are free, one comes with a paid plan, and two run on paying readers.

## What are the best marketing newsletters for brand marketers?

The best marketing newsletters for brand marketers pair a clear audience with a steady schedule. I checked each one's price and cadence against its own site in October 2026.

- **Marketing Brew:** free, every weekday, for brand and ad news with the reasoning.
- **Link in Bio:** free weekly interview, paid tier at $8 a month, for social media teams.
- **Snaxshot:** reader funded, no ads, for food and CPG trend-spotting.
- **The Sizzle:** in Event Marketer Pro, $299 a year, for event and experiential marketers.
- **Why We Buy:** free, every Tuesday, for anyone who writes the ask.

## Marketing Brew

Marketing Brew comes from Morning Brew Inc. and covers brand strategy, social media, and ad tech every weekday. Its [subscribe page](https://www.marketingbrew.com/subscribe) says it is free.

The [September 23, 2026 story on Inter Milan](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/inter-milan-social-strategy) covered a club that cut its social posting by about a third in a year. Impressions rose 43 percent and video views 33 percent, per the club, and followers grew 20 percent year over year. Brand and marketing director Luca Adornato called it quality over quantity, with a plan for each platform. The club passed 100 million followers in early August.

Give each platform its own plan, then post less.

## Link in Bio

Rachel Karten writes Link in Bio from Los Angeles. She led the social team at Bon Appétit, and her About page counts more than 100,000 subscribers. The free issue is a weekly interview with, in her words, "the people who actually press post." The paid tier adds a weekly strategy send and quarterly trend reports.

The [September 17, 2026 issue](https://www.linkinbio.news/p/ikea-reactive-social-strategy) interviewed Elissa Wardrop, IKEA's global social media specialist. Her Responsive team meets two to three times a week and runs each post through a checklist. One test: if another brand could put its logo on the post and it still works, it is not unique enough.

The lesson: run that logo test before anyone opens a design file.

## Snaxshot

Andrea Hernández writes Snaxshot on Substack about "what's coming next" in food and beverage. A [2024 Adweek profile](https://www.adweek.com/media/substack-snaxshot-food-and-beverage/) called it the antidote to food and beverage puff pieces. It runs with no ads and no PR pitches, funded by readers. It covers the category behind my [Fishwife breakdown](/blog/fishwife-cult-following).

The [June 10, 2026 essay "The New Grocer"](https://www.snaxshot.com/p/the-new-grocer-1e4) traced grocery from the general store to Piggly Wiggly's self-service aisles to Erewhon. It described slotting fees as grocers selling their best shelf space to the highest bidder.

A prime shelf spot shows what a brand paid. I read placement as budget first.

## The Sizzle from Event Marketer

The Sizzle is Event Marketer's Friday newsletter on "what's up in experiential marketing this week." Event Marketer's [subscribe page](https://www.eventmarketer.com/subscribe/) lists it in the Pro plan at $299 a year. Each issue rounds up the week's activations. Its coverage fed two entries on my [list of 2026 brand activations](/blog/10-brand-moments-people-showed-up-for-2026).

The [September 18, 2026 issue](https://www.eventmarketer.com/eletters/em-sizzle-newsletter-9-18-2026/) led with HomeGoods' "Real Big Deal" in Chicago's Pioneer Court. Giant everyday items carried QR codes for a $5 rideshare coupon toward a HomeGoods trip.

A photo op without a next step ends at the photo.

## Why We Buy

Katelyn Bourgoin writes Why We Buy, a free weekly that lands on Tuesdays. Its [signup page](https://learnwhywebuy.com/) claims more than 63,000 readers. Each issue takes one bias from behavioral science and ends with ways to apply it.

The [February 17, 2026 issue](https://learnwhywebuy.com/warm-glow-giving/) covered Warm-Glow Giving, the good feeling people get from generosity. It pointed to the ASPCA's $19-a-month ask, which casts the donor as the reason an animal gets a second chance.

I write donor materials for a scholarship program, a role I describe in [my first-gen career story](/blog/first-gen-and-figuring-it-out). The lesson: make the donor the hero of the ask. I would write the next Hope Scholars donor ask that way.

## How many marketing newsletters should you subscribe to?

Subscribe to as many as you will finish in a week. The four on this list with a fixed schedule send nine issues a week. That is five from Marketing Brew, two from Link in Bio, and one each from The Sizzle and Why We Buy. I would start with two, one for news and one for mechanics, and add a third only when both get read.
