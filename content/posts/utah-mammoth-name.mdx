Utah's NHL team wanted to be the Yetis, but the U.S. trademark office turned the name down because of Yeti, the cooler brand. Its fans had already been voting on names for nine months by then, and more than 850,000 votes later, they gave the team its permanent name: the Utah Mammoth.

The fan vote is the part worth studying. The Utah Mammoth brand shows what happens when a team understands its audience before it builds anything, and then uses its launch to show that audience why the choice fits.

## How did the Utah Mammoth get its name?

The team played its inaugural 2024–25 season as the Utah Hockey Club, a temporary name it retired once the permanent one arrived, [The Salt Lake Tribune reported](https://www.sltrib.com/sports/utah-hockey-club/2025/05/07/utah-mammoth-is-new-name-utah). The search for that permanent name started in April 2024 with a public call for ideas, [KSL reported](https://www.ksl.com/article/51308328). It ran for 13 months, through four rounds of fan voting and more than 850,000 votes, according to the [team's announcement](https://www.nhl.com/utah/news/utah-s-nhl-franchise-officially-named-the-utah-mammoth-release-5-7-25). By June 2024, fans had cut a list of 20 names down to six finalists: Blizzard, Hockey Club, Mammoth, Outlaws, Venom, and Yeti, [The Athletic reported](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6339788/2025/05/07/utah-mammoth-team-name-nhl/).

The Yeti detour came in January 2025, when the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office rejected the name over a "likelihood of confusion" with Yeti Coolers, [Sports Illustrated reported](https://www.si.com/nhl/utah-hockey-club-fans-vote-three-possible-nicknames-yeti-moniker-rejected). The team moved on with three finalists, Mammoth, Wasatch, and Hockey Club, and fans voted on tablets at four home games.

Mammoth had a neighbor of its own. The National Lacrosse League already has a Colorado Mammoth, and its owner, Kroenke Sports & Entertainment, supported Utah adopting the name, according to the team, [The Athletic reported](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6339788/2025/05/07/utah-mammoth-team-name-nhl/). As I read it, the two names show both sides of naming a brand. A name fans love only works if you can own it, and a name someone else already uses can still work when that owner agrees.

When the result came in, owners Ryan and Ashley Smith gave the credit to the voters: "The community chose the Utah Mammoth, and it stands as a symbol of who we are, where we came from, and the unstoppable force we're building together." By reveal day, the name already belonged to the people who had picked it. The team still had to introduce it.

## What happens in the "Introducing Utah Mammoth" film?

The team revealed the name on May 7, 2025, with a short film called "Introducing Utah Mammoth." Its YouTube caption reads "A new Ice Age dawns."

<YouTube id="Qq1JOr4EmD8" title="Introducing Utah Mammoth" />

The film follows two young museum-goers and a hiker who uncovers a tusk deep underground, [DesignRush reported](https://news.designrush.com/utah-mammoth-launch-film-reinvents-sports-marketing-mythic-brand), and it cuts between them as the hints build. It ends with a tusk breaking through the ice and the team's new rally cry, "Tusks Up." Field Work Creative, a Salt Lake City production company, made it on a tight clock. "We had eight days to pull this off, from permits to mountain roads," director Zeppelin Zeerip told DesignRush.

The film never explains the name. It puts the discovery on screen and trusts you to connect it to Utah, and that works because the connection is real.

## Why does the mammoth fit Utah?

Herds of mammoths lived in Utah more than 10,000 years ago, and the team's announcement lists fossil finds in Bear Lake, Fillmore, Orem, Park City, and Lake Powell. The best-known find came in 1988 in Huntington Canyon. Researchers described that Columbian mammoth in the [Journal of Paleontology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-paleontology/article/abs/columbian-mammoth-mammuthus-columbi-from-the-wasatch-mountains-of-central-utah/9DEB9E0C492528318D4FC8A8F58A35B0) as a nearly complete, well-preserved skeleton, found near the crest of the Wasatch Plateau.

That history gave the team a name with roots in the state instead of one invented for merchandise. As I see it, this is why the vote and the film worked together. The fans picked the name, and the film showed them why it already belonged to Utah.

## What does the Utah Mammoth logo mean?

The primary logo, called the Mountain Mammoth, shows the animal "mid-charge, tusks up, and ready to attack," according to the team. Utah is built into the details: the Wasatch Range and its snow-capped peaks form the mammoth's crown, the shape of the state is hidden in the mountain silhouette, and the curved tusk forms a "U." The team developed the identity with design firm Doubleday & Cartwright.

The colors carry the same idea. Rock Black stands for the mountains at night, Salt White for the snow on Utah's peaks and the salt flats, and Mountain Blue for the state's winter sports history and its clear skies for more than 230 days a year. Every piece of the identity points back to a place fans already know.

## What this means for marketers

The team could have picked a name in a meeting and spent its launch convincing fans to like it. Instead, it spent 13 months asking them. By the time the film went up on May 7, 2025, more than 850,000 votes had been cast, so the people watching had already helped build the brand.

That changed the job of the launch. The film only had to give the name a story, and the logo backed it up with details fans could find on their own: the peaks, the shape of the state, the "U" in the tusk.

You can try the same approach on a smaller launch. Before you name a product or rebrand a program, ask the people you want to reach what they would choose, and look for a story they already know. Then build the launch around their answer.

The film made the reveal look good. The vote made it theirs.
