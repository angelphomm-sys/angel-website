Shyft is a product development and supply chain partner in Orem, Utah. From March to August 2025 I managed its social channels, LinkedIn, Instagram, and YouTube, for an audience that is not scrolling for fun: operations managers, founders, and sourcing leads deciding who to trust with a product launch.

That audience shaped everything. Two formats did most of the work.

## Format one: the employee highlight

<LinkCard href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shyft-global_evan-loomis-industrial-designer-activity-7344418875712684036-Yk02" label="LinkedIn · Shyft" title="Meet Evan Loomis, industrial designer" />

Business-to-business (B2B) buyers hire people, not logos. The highlight series put a face on each stage of Shyft's process, here, industrial designer Evan Loomis, who takes a concept from sketch through material selection to something that resonates with the end user.

The structure I used for every profile:

1. **What they do for the client**, in one sentence a buyer would care about.
2. **How they think**, one specific detail about their process (Evan's focus on material selection and refinement).
3. **Who they are** off the clock (rock climbing, Star Wars theory debates), so the post reads as human, not HR.
4. **A soft call to action (CTA)** inviting brands to partner with that person on their next launch.

## Format two: the Friday Market Roundup

<LinkCard href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shyft-global_supplychain-tariffs-globaltrade-activity-7306354271824883713-fiKO" label="LinkedIn · Shyft" title="Friday Market Roundup: tariffs and global trade" />

Spring 2025 was a tariff news cycle, 10% on Chinese goods becoming 20%, 25% on Canadian and Mexican goods with USMCA exemptions under review, new duties on steel and aluminum. Shyft's customers needed to understand it fast. We built a recurring video series with Ben Reynolds, VP of Revenue, breaking down what changed that week and what an agile supply chain does about it.

Why a series and not one post: a weekly slot trains the audience to expect you. The hashtags (#supplychain #tariffs #globaltrade) did the discovery; the consistency did the retention.

## What I actually did

- Wrote and produced the educational content, including scripting and cutting the recurring video series.
- Managed the calendar across LinkedIn, Instagram, and YouTube, adapting each piece per platform.
- Proposed, tested, and led new creative initiatives alongside the sales team, so content answered the questions reps were hearing on calls.

## What I'd do differently

- **Lead with the number.** The tariff posts opened with context; the version that would stop a scroll opens with "20%."
- **Cut the roundup shorter.** Thirty to forty-five seconds, one takeaway, then the link.
- **Build the highlight series into a carousel** so each teammate's process could be shown, not just described.
