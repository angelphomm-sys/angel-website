A line around the block makes a great photo. It doesn't prove the event worked.

In June 2026, Rhode, Hailey Bieber's beauty brand, opened the first stop of its summer pop-up tour in Newport, Rhode Island. Rhode had warned online that "wait times may be long," and people still waited seven to ten hours, [Inc. reported](https://www.inc.com/annabel-burba/unorganized-unprofessional-and-upsetting-hailey-bieber-founded-rhodes-crowded-pop-up-sparks-backlash-online/91362071). Some reached the front after the product they came for had sold out, and many posted about it on TikTok. The photos showed a hit. The guests told a different story.

Experiential marketing means giving people something to do with a brand in person, not just an ad to watch. Judging it takes two things: what the brand wanted from the event, and what it reported afterward. The seven experiential marketing examples below, from 2024 to 2026, show both, and they lead to one rule: decide what success looks like before you plan anything else. If you plan pop-ups, fundraisers, or game days on a small team, you can borrow the thinking without the budget.

| Brand | Event | What it wanted | What it reported |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | NYC pop-up, 2024 | Make its mascot famous | A sell-out target set in advance, no published result |
| Bloom Nutrition | Coachella 7-Eleven, 2026 | Awareness and buzz | Said it would judge by chatter, not foot traffic |
| Rhode | Summer pop-up tour, 2026 | Launch a summer collection | No numbers, but a visible fix by the next stop |
| Sincerely Yours | Launch pop-up, 2025 | Launch a new brand | 80,000-plus visitors, about 45,000 TikToks |

## 1. Set the target before the doors open

In August 2024, Duolingo opened a three-day pop-up shop in New York, and the price of entry was a Duolingo lesson, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/duolingo-wants-to-make-its-green-owl-mascot-as-famous-as-pikachu-with-its-first-pop-up-store/). Inside, a board tracked guests' streaks, and every 30 minutes the longest streak won a jumbo plush of Duo, the owl mascot. The bigger goal was to make Duo "as famous as Pikachu," so sales came second.

What sets this example apart is the scorecard. Before opening, Duolingo said the shop would be a success if it sold out of about 7,000 items, and it expected 3,500 to 5,000 visitors. I couldn't find a published final count, but the bar was public before the doors opened, and that is rarer than it should be.

**The lesson:** Charge people the action you want most, like trying the product or signing up, and write the target down before the event starts. For more on how Duolingo turns its owl into a strategy, see my [Duolingo breakdown](/blog/duolingo-social-media-strategy).

## 2. Meet people where they're already going

For Coachella weekend in April 2026, Bloom Nutrition wrapped a 7-Eleven near the Palm Springs airport in pink, with DJs, samples, and an airbrush merch station, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/bloom-pacsun-and-more-chase-coachella-crowds-on-the-way-to-the-show-with-roadside-activations/). CEO Greg LaVecchia called it "the ultimate way to break up a road trip."

The detail most coverage skipped is the store. Bloom's energy drinks were launching in 7-Eleven stores nationwide, so festivalgoers met the brand inside the chain where they could soon buy it. Bloom said it would measure the stop less by foot traffic and financial return than by awareness, social chatter, and virality.

**The lesson:** Even an awareness event needs a next step. Host it where your product is sold, and the next step is built in.

## 3. Match the line to the stock

Rhode's Newport stop shows what happens when demand outruns supply. Staff handed out wristbands from the front of the line each morning, and the brand reportedly had only 36 of its $110 Rhode Island-themed hoodies, Inc. reported. A long wait wasn't the real problem. A long wait that ended at a sold-out sign was.

Rhode never shared attendance or sales numbers, but its next stop showed a change. Soon after the Dallas pop-up opened in late June, Rhode posted on Instagram Stories that it was "testing out timed entry to help manage capacity and keep your wait time in the sun as short as possible," [D Magazine reported](https://www.dmagazine.com/style-beauty-wellness/2026/06/rhode-dallas-summer-station-pop-up/). By the next stop, two weeks later, Rhode was already testing a fix.

**The lesson:** If supply is limited, match your wristbands or tickets to your stock, not just your space. Then count how many people left without what they came for, and fix that number at the next event.

## 4. Build a moment people want to post

Sincerely Yours, a skincare brand for teens, launched with a two-day pop-up at the American Dream mall in New Jersey in September 2025. The mall estimated more than 80,000 people came over the weekend, and about 45,000 TikTok videos were posted about the event, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/over-80000-attend-launch-pop-up-of-teen-skin-care-brand-sincerely-yours/).

Those 45,000 videos are the real result. Each one carried the launch to people who were never at the mall, at no cost to the brand. A crowd fills a room once, but a moment worth filming keeps reaching people for weeks.

**The lesson:** Plan the shot before you plan the floor. Build one photo spot or interactive moment guests will want to share, then count the posts afterward.

## 5. Get a name before they leave

Bloom ran a different kind of event in September 2025. At its Treat Shoppe pop-up in SoHo, it launched its community on TYB, a fan rewards app, and set up an on-site sign-up booth. TYB's [case study](https://www.tyb.xyz/case-studies/bloom-activates-community-at-exclusive-irl-experiences-driving-68-growth-during-treat-shoppe-week) reports that 5,500 people joined Bloom's community in the first month and 13,000 signed up for email. Those are the vendor's numbers, but the structure is the lesson.

Compare it with Coachella. The 7-Eleven stop bought awareness, and the Treat Shoppe left Bloom with people it could reach again the next week. Same brand, two events, two very different things to show for them.

**The lesson:** Give guests a reason to leave their contact info, like early access or a discount, and a fast way to do it, such as a QR code at the exit. A follower is rented attention, but an email address is yours. I cover more of Bloom's event strategy in my [Bloom breakdown](/blog/bloom-nutrition-marketing-strategy).

## 6. Judge the event by what happens after it

On May 18, 2024, Graza threw a free afternoon at McCarren Parkhouse in Brooklyn, where fans could bring their empty squeeze bottles for free olive oil refills, [Time Out reported](https://www.timeout.com/newyork/things-to-do/refill-chill-with-graza). It marked the launch of Graza's refill cans, which went on sale at select Whole Foods stores nationwide and on its site, [Fast Company reported](https://www.fastcompany.com/91121437/graza-olive-oil-beer-can-refill).

Graza never published numbers from the event itself. The clearest result came almost a year later, when CEO Andrew Benin told [Nosh](https://www.nosh.com/news/2025/a-bite-with-graza-ceo-andrew-benin) that the refills "could not be going better," with online adoption "incredibly strong" and more retailers carrying them.

**The lesson:** An event is a starting point. Judge it by what people do in the weeks after, like reordering, signing up, or coming back. More on Graza's launch strategy is in my [Graza breakdown](/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza).

## 7. Design for first-timers

Soft Life Ski is a festival built to bring Black and ethnic minority communities to the slopes. Its 2025 trip to LAAX, Switzerland, welcomed more than 900 guests, and the resort said 82 percent were skiing or snowboarding for the first time, [SnowsportsNews reported](https://www.snowsportsnews.com/articles/soft-life-ski-afro-caribbean-festival-returns-to-laax). The trip sold out in seven minutes, and the festival set its 2026 edition for 1,500 guests.

The festival plans for the people who have never done this before. For an indoor slope day at The Snow Centre in Hemel Hempstead, outside London, guests rode free buses from East London, [Indoor Snow News reported](https://indoorsnownews.com/2026/01/01/soft-life-ski-takes-over-indoor-slope/). Removing the trip there removes one more reason to stay home.

**The lesson:** People try something new when they know they'll belong. Lead with who they'll be with, remove the barriers you can, and count how many first-timers come back.

## How to measure experiential marketing

Every example above comes back to the same move: pick the one result that would prove the event worked, before you plan anything else. Duolingo picked a sell-out. Bloom picked awareness once and contacts the next time. Rhode found its number the hard way, in a ten-hour line.

I work this way at UVU Athletics, where I help stage game-day activations. Each campaign ends with a report against the target we set at the start, so the plan is built backward from that number. Before your next event, borrow one question from each brand:

- **Duolingo:** What would you call a success, in numbers, before the doors open?
- **Bloom at Coachella:** Where are people already going, and can you meet them there?
- **Rhode:** What will you track at this event to make the next one better?
- **Sincerely Yours:** What will people want to post?
- **Bloom at the Treat Shoppe:** How will you reach guests again next week?
- **Graza:** What should people keep doing after the event ends?
- **Soft Life Ski:** Who is new, and what might stop them from coming?

Then write the answer to the first question at the top of the plan. If your goal is new volunteers, the number is how many people sign up before they leave. The line around the block is still a great photo. Make sure it's also proof.

## FAQ

**What is experiential marketing?**
Experiential marketing gives people something to do with a brand in person, like a pop-up, a sampling event, or a game-day activation, instead of only showing them an ad. The goal is a memory and a next step, not just an impression.

**How do you measure experiential marketing?**
Set one target before the event, tied to what you want people to do: sign-ups, sales, social posts, or repeat visits. Then count that number during and after the event, and compare it with the target.
