A line around the block makes a great photo. It doesn't prove the event worked.

In June 2026, Rhode, Hailey Bieber's beauty brand, opened the first stop of its summer pop-up tour in Newport, Rhode Island. Rhode had warned online that "wait times may be long," and people still waited seven to ten hours, [Inc. reported](https://www.inc.com/annabel-burba/unorganized-unprofessional-and-upsetting-hailey-bieber-founded-rhodes-crowded-pop-up-sparks-backlash-online/91362071). Some reached the front after the product they came for had sold out, and many posted about it on TikTok. The photos showed a hit. The guests told a different story.

Experiential marketing means giving people something to do with a brand in person, not just an ad to watch. Judging it takes two things: what the brand wanted from the event, and what it reported afterward. The seven experiential marketing examples below, from 2024 to 2026, show both, and together they point to one rule: decide what success looks like before you plan anything else. If you plan pop-ups, fundraisers, or game days on a small team, you can borrow the thinking without the budget.

## 1. Duolingo set its target before the doors opened

In August 2024, Duolingo opened a three-day pop-up shop in New York, and the price of entry was a Duolingo lesson, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/duolingo-wants-to-make-its-green-owl-mascot-as-famous-as-pikachu-with-its-first-pop-up-store/). Inside, a board tracked guests' streaks, and every 30 minutes the longest streak won a jumbo plush of Duo, the owl mascot. The company described its larger goal as making Duo "as famous as Pikachu."

Duolingo also set its scorecard before opening. The shop would count as a success if it sold out of about 7,000 items, and the company expected 3,500 to 5,000 visitors. I couldn't find a published final count, but the target was public before the first guest walked in. That's the habit worth borrowing: charge people the action you most want from them, like a lesson or a sign-up, and write the number down first. My [Duolingo breakdown](/blog/duolingo-social-media-strategy) covers how the brand built Duo into a strategy.

## 2. Bloom met festivalgoers where they could buy

For Coachella weekend in April 2026, Bloom Nutrition wrapped a 7-Eleven near the Palm Springs airport in pink, with DJs, samples, and an airbrush merch station, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/bloom-pacsun-and-more-chase-coachella-crowds-on-the-way-to-the-show-with-roadside-activations/). CEO Greg LaVecchia called it "the ultimate way to break up a road trip."

The location did more than set the scene. Bloom's energy drinks were launching in 7-Eleven stores nationwide, so festivalgoers met the brand inside the chain where they could soon buy it. Bloom said it would measure the stop less by foot traffic and financial return than by awareness, social chatter, and virality. Even an awareness event can carry a next step, and hosting it where the product is sold builds that step in.

## 3. Rhode learned to match the line to the stock

Back in Newport, demand outran supply. Staff handed out wristbands from the front of the line each morning, and the brand reportedly had only 36 of its $110 Rhode Island-themed hoodies, according to Inc. The way I read it, the long wait wasn't the real problem. A long wait that ended at a sold-out sign was.

Rhode never shared attendance or sales numbers, but its next stop showed a change. Soon after the Dallas pop-up opened in late June, Rhode posted on Instagram Stories that it was "testing out timed entry to help manage capacity and keep your wait time in the sun as short as possible," [D Magazine reported](https://www.dmagazine.com/style-beauty-wellness/2026/06/rhode-dallas-summer-station-pop-up/). When supply is limited, tickets or wristbands should match the stock, not just the space, and the number worth tracking is how many people left without what they came for.

## 4. Sincerely Yours built a launch people filmed

Sincerely Yours, a teen skincare brand, launched with a two-day pop-up at the American Dream mall in New Jersey in September 2025. The mall estimated more than 80,000 people came over the weekend, and about 45,000 TikTok videos were posted about the event, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/over-80000-attend-launch-pop-up-of-teen-skin-care-brand-sincerely-yours/).

To me, those videos are the result that matters most, because each one carried the launch to people who were never at the mall. A crowd fills a room once, while a moment worth filming keeps reaching people after the doors close. That's why the photo spot or interactive moment belongs in the plan before the floor layout, and why the posts deserve a count afterward.

## 5. Bloom collected names at the Treat Shoppe

Bloom ran a different kind of event in September 2025. At its Treat Shoppe pop-up in SoHo, it launched its community on TYB, a fan rewards app, and set up an on-site sign-up booth. TYB's [case study](https://www.tyb.xyz/case-studies/bloom-activates-community-at-exclusive-irl-experiences-driving-68-growth-during-treat-shoppe-week) reports that 5,500 people joined Bloom's community in the first month and 13,000 signed up for email. Those are the vendor's numbers, but the setup is easy to borrow: give guests a reason to leave their contact info, like early access or a discount, and a fast way to do it, such as a QR code at the exit.

Set next to Coachella, the difference is clear. The 7-Eleven stop built awareness, and the Treat Shoppe left Bloom with people it could reach again the following week. My [Bloom breakdown](/blog/bloom-nutrition-marketing-strategy) goes deeper on how the brand runs its events.

## 6. Graza's refill day paid off months later

On May 18, 2024, Graza hosted a free afternoon at McCarren Parkhouse in Brooklyn, where fans could bring their empty squeeze bottles for free olive oil refills, [Time Out reported](https://www.timeout.com/newyork/things-to-do/refill-chill-with-graza). It marked the launch of Graza's refill cans, which went on sale at select Whole Foods stores nationwide and on its site, [Fast Company reported](https://www.fastcompany.com/91121437/graza-olive-oil-beer-can-refill).

Graza never published numbers from the event itself. The clearest result came almost a year later, when CEO Andrew Benin told [Nosh](https://www.nosh.com/news/2025/a-bite-with-graza-ceo-andrew-benin) that the refills "could not be going better," with online adoption "incredibly strong" and "more and more retailers" carrying them. An event's real test often shows up weeks or months later, in reorders, sign-ups, and return visits. The [Graza breakdown](/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza) covers the rest of its launch strategy.

## 7. Soft Life Ski designed for first-timers

Soft Life Ski is a festival built to bring Black and ethnic minority communities to the slopes. Its 2025 trip to LAAX, Switzerland, welcomed more than 900 guests, and the resort said 82 percent were skiing or snowboarding for the first time, [SnowsportsNews reported](https://www.snowsportsnews.com/articles/soft-life-ski-afro-caribbean-festival-returns-to-laax). The trip sold out in seven minutes, and the festival set its 2026 edition for 1,500 guests.

The planning for newcomers shows up in the details. For an indoor slope day at The Snow Centre in Hemel Hempstead, outside London, guests rode free buses from East London, [Indoor Snow News reported](https://indoorsnownews.com/2026/01/01/soft-life-ski-takes-over-indoor-slope/). People try something new when they know they'll belong and the trip there is handled, and the number worth tracking is how many first-timers come back.

## How to measure experiential marketing

Every example above comes back to one move: pick the result that would prove the event worked before you plan anything else. Duolingo picked a sell-out. Bloom picked awareness at Coachella and contacts at the Treat Shoppe. Rhode found its number the hard way, in a ten-hour line, and adjusted about two weeks later.

I work this way at UVU Athletics, where I help stage game-day activations, and each campaign ends with a report against the target set at the start. My Pi Mai Lao concept, [Embracing Our Story](/case/embracing-our-story), was built the same way, with every piece ending on one action: reserve a free spot. For more crowd-first ideas, see the [best brand activations of 2026](/blog/10-brand-moments-people-showed-up-for-2026).

Before your next event, borrow one question from each brand:

- **Duolingo:** What number would count as a success before the doors open?
- **Bloom at Coachella:** Where are people already going, and can you meet them there?
- **Rhode:** What will you track at this event to make the next one better?
- **Sincerely Yours:** What will people want to post?
- **Bloom at the Treat Shoppe:** How will you reach guests again next week?
- **Graza:** What should people keep doing after the event ends?
- **Soft Life Ski:** Who is new, and what might stop them from coming?

Answer the first question at the top of the plan. If your goal is new volunteers, the number is how many people sign up before they leave. The line around the block is still a great photo. Make sure it's also proof.
