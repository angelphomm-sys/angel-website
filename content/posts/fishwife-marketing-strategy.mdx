Fishwife's marketing strategy usually gets told as a packaging story: pretty tins, no ads, and TikTok did the rest. The tin did win the first look. But a steady run of collaborations is what kept the brand growing from a December 2020 launch to about 5,000 stores, and that is the part a small brand can copy.

**The short version**

- **The tin came first.** Founder Becca Millstein built the brand identity before the product, so every photo of a tin works like a free ad.
- **"No paid ads" is a 2022 snapshot.** A co-founder's following and influencer seeding powered it, and Millstein said then that it would not last.
- **Collaborations do the reaching now.** Fishwife runs several a month, Millstein said in January 2025.
- **Sourcing backs up the brand, but taste and humor lead the message.**

## Why did Fishwife design the tin before sourcing the fish?

Fishwife started with the look and filled the tin second. Millstein told [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/fishwife-success-tinned-fish/726922/) in September 2024 that she had already hired illustrator Danny Miller and "created the initial brand identity" before the first sample box was filled. Food Dive credited the "distinctive, vibrant and colorful tins" with setting Fishwife apart from the European brands Millstein had been buying.

That order makes sense once you think about where tinned fish gets eaten: on a table, usually with a phone nearby. A tin that looks good gets photographed, and every photo carries the brand to people who have never heard of it, at no cost to Fishwife. Millstein put it plainly to Help Scout in 2023: "We built Fishwife on Instagram." The design is what made that possible, which means the tin did the job a launch ad would normally do. Graza made the same bet with a squeeze bottle, as I cover in the [Graza breakdown](/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza).

Fishwife's own TikTok from June 2024 opens with "HI WE'RE FISHWIFE." Its hashtags, #tinnedfish and #tinnedfishtok, show where the brand wanted to be found.

<TikTok url="https://www.tiktok.com/@eatfishwife/video/7385273553224092971" title="Fishwife introduction TikTok, June 2024" />

**Steal this:** if your product ends up on a table, desk, or counter, design it for the photo before you spend anything on reach.

## The name and the sourcing do quieter work

The name pulls the same trick in words. "Fishwife" was once a gendered insult aimed at the wives and daughters of 16th-century fishermen, [Secret NYC reported](https://secretnyc.co/fishwife-nyc-pop-up/) in October 2024, and the female-founded brand took it back and put it on every tin. The product names stay literal, so Smoked Rainbow Trout with Red Chimichurri tells you exactly what is in the can. The wit lives in the brand name, and the clarity lives on the label.

Sourcing gets a lighter touch than you might expect from a seafood brand. Fishwife's [Our Story page](https://eatfishwife.com/pages/our-story) says its fish comes from Marine Stewardship Council or Aquaculture Stewardship Council certified fisheries, and it names six canneries across Washington State, Spain, Denmark, and Scotland. But Millstein told Marketing Brew in April 2022 that responsible sourcing was "table stakes these days," and that the main branding strategy was "messaging deliciousness, humor, exuberance, artfulness."

So the certifications sit on the website for the shopper who checks, while the tins and the posts sell taste. If every competitor in your category makes the same claim, put it in your proof, because it will not set you apart in a headline.

## Did Fishwife really grow without paid ads?

At first, yes, and this is the part of the story I think gets misread. [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2022/04/07/fishwife-made-its-presence-known-organically-now-it-s-thinking-bigger) reported in April 2022 that Fishwife had "not done any paid marketing." That line gets repeated as proof that great packaging is enough on its own.

The same article explains why it worked. Millstein credited much of the early growth to co-founder Caroline Goldfarb, saying Goldfarb's followers "helped things move really, really fast." The brand was also sending product to influencers and micro-influencers. So the free reach had an engine behind it, an audience that existed on day one plus steady seeding, and Millstein said in that interview that the organic growth was "not gonna last forever."

While it lasted, that engine was strong. Millstein told [Help Scout](https://intheworks.helpscout.com/use-social-media-to-grow-business/) in 2023 that 40 percent of customers discover Fishwife through social media. The #tinnedfishdatenight hashtag had 7.4 million TikTok views by September 2022, [Bustle reported](https://www.bustle.com/life/tinned-fish-date-night-tiktok-trend), and Fishwife was one of the brands it named. Fishwife's own TikTok from May 2023 opens with "welcome to tinned fish tok," which shows the brand talking to a community that was already forming on the app.

The lesson I take from this is narrower than "skip ads." No paid marketing worked for a brand that launched with a built-in audience and a seeding habit. If you launch without a co-founder's following, budget for seeding or paid reach from the start instead of hoping the packaging travels alone. ## Why collaborations drive Fishwife's marketing strategy

Millstein's own plan pointed away from big ad budgets. In April 2022, she told Marketing Brew she was "much less interested in just shoveling $10,000 into Facebook ads." By January 2025, she told [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.com.au/sectors/fishwifes-becca-millstein-talks-brand-journey-social-media-and-subscriptions-202501) that Fishwife runs collaborations "several a month" across food, fashion, and jewelry.

This is my central read of Fishwife: the collab calendar does the job a media budget does at most brands. Each partner brings its own audience and puts the tin somewhere new, whether that is a chili crisp fan's pantry, a sandal display, or a salad bowl. Because these collabs are physical things people can hold, they get posted the same way the tins do, so each one keeps paying out after launch day.

Two of these show how far the idea stretches. The Fly By Jing smoked salmon launched as a co-branded tin in March 2022, and it is still listed among the canneries on Fishwife's Our Story page in 2026, so a limited collab became a lasting product. The Sweetgreen Niçoise came with a custom co-branded tin of Fishwife Albacore Tuna with Spanish Lemon, which put the brand in front of salad-chain customers who may never walk down the tinned fish aisle. For a smaller brand, the takeaway is to plan collaborations as a calendar with a reach goal for each one, instead of treating them as one-off favors.

## Retail followed the attention

Stores came after the audience, and the timeline shows it. Whole Foods announced a nationwide Fishwife expansion in April 2024 with two exclusive albacore tuna products, after conversations that began in 2021, The Metal Packager reported. Fishwife was in 1,800 retail locations that month, per [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/12/fishwife-founder-becca-millstein-tinned-fish), and in 5,000 brick-and-mortar retailers by January 2025, per Inside Retail.

In January 2026, [Sporked reported](https://sporked.com/article/fishwife-tinned-fish-costco-news/) a national Costco rollout, with a 3-pack of Albacore in Spicy Olive Oil at about $15, while 3-packs on Fishwife's own site go for over $30. A brand that grew on giftable, premium tins now also sells warehouse packs at about half its online price, which suggests it is reaching shoppers who never saw the TikToks. I'm watching whether the collabs can keep the brand feeling special while Costco brings in volume.

## What to try if you are building a small brand

The parts of Fishwife's strategy that travel are the order and the calendar: design the thing people will photograph before you spend on reach, then line up partners whose audiences overlap with yours.

At Green House, the clients I work with are small and mid-size local businesses, and this is the move I'd test first with them. Pick two nearby brands whose customers look like yours, make one limited product or event together, and track how many new followers or email sign-ups each partner sends your way. If one collab brings in more new people than a month of regular posting, you have found your media plan.
