In 2015, Becca Millstein was living in Spain, taken with the Spanish and Portuguese tinned seafood called conservas, and she believed American cupboards had room for fish that good. Five years later, she and Caroline Goldfarb launched Fishwife. Its marketing strategy usually gets told as a packaging story, and the tin did win the first look. But a steady calendar of collaborations is what carried the brand from a December 2020 launch to about 5,000 stores, and that is the part a small brand can copy.

**The short version**

- **The tin came first.** Millstein built the brand identity before the product, so every photo of a tin works like a free ad.
- **"No paid ads" is a 2022 snapshot.** A co-founder's following and steady influencer gifting powered it, and Millstein said then that it would not last.
- **Collaborations do the reaching now.** Fishwife runs several a month, Millstein said in January 2025, and the tin itself has become the collab format.
- **The catch:** partners said yes because Fishwife already had attention, so the collab calendar is something you earn, and you cannot start with it.

## What gap did Fishwife's founder see in tinned fish?

Fishwife's [Our Story page](https://eatfishwife.com/pages/our-story) says the idea started in 2015, when Millstein was living in Spain and fell for the culture around Spanish and Portuguese conservas. She believed there was a home for tinned fish of the same quality in the United States, and in 2020 she started Fishwife with Caroline Goldfarb. The stated mission is to make premium, sustainably sourced tinned seafood "a staple in every cupboard."

The category itself was barely growing. Millstein said the conventional tinned fish category grew just 1 percent from 2022 to 2023, in the company's April 2024 Whole Foods announcement covered by [The Metal Packager](https://metalpackager.com/2024/04/fast-growing-tinned-fish-brand-expands-across-us/). The good stuff existed, but it came from European brands, which is what Millstein had been buying before she started her own, Food Dive reported. So the opening I see was in how American tinned fish looked and felt. In my read, it sat in the pantry as backup food, and Fishwife set out to make it something you would want to put on the table.

## Why did Fishwife design the tin before sourcing the fish?

Fishwife started with the look and filled the tin second. Millstein told [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/fishwife-success-tinned-fish/726922/) in September 2024 that she had already hired illustrator Danny Miller and "created the initial brand identity" before the first sample box was filled. Food Dive credited the "distinctive, vibrant and colorful tins" with setting Fishwife apart from the European brands Millstein had been buying.

That order makes sense once you think about where tinned fish gets eaten: on a table, usually with a phone nearby. A tin that looks good gets photographed, and every photo carries the brand to people who have never heard of it, at no cost to Fishwife. Millstein put it plainly to [Help Scout](https://intheworks.helpscout.com/use-social-media-to-grow-business/) in September 2023: "We built Fishwife on Instagram." The design is what made that possible, which means the tin did the job a launch ad would normally do. Graza made the same bet with a squeeze bottle, as I cover in the [Graza breakdown](/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza).

The illustrator never changed, either. Sweetgreen's August 2026 release credits Miller with the artwork on its co-branded tin, so six years in, the look still comes from the same hand. Fishwife's own TikTok from June 2024 opens with "HI WE'RE FISHWIFE," and its hashtags, #tinnedfish and #tinnedfishtok, show where the brand wanted to be found.

<TikTok url="https://www.tiktok.com/@eatfishwife/video/7385273553224092971" title="Fishwife introduction TikTok, June 2024" />

**Steal this:** if your product ends up on a table, desk, or counter, design it for the photo before you spend anything on reach.

## The name and the sourcing do quieter work

The name pulls the same trick in words. "Fishwife" was once a gendered insult aimed at the wives and daughters of 16th-century fishermen, [Secret NYC reported](https://secretnyc.co/fishwife-nyc-pop-up/) in October 2024, and the female-founded brand took it back and put it on every tin. The product names stay literal, so Smoked Rainbow Trout with Red Chimichurri tells you exactly what is in the can. The wit lives in the brand name, and the clarity lives on the label.

<Figure src="/images/blog/fishwife-marketing-strategy/fishwife-smoked-trout-red-chimichurri-tin.jpg" alt="A lime green Fishwife tin labeled Smoked Trout with Red Chimichurri, decorated with pink flowers and the illustrated Fishwife woman" caption="The label states the fish and the sauce in plain words, while the flowers and the character carry the personality. Image: Fishwife" ratio="4/3" />

Sourcing gets a lighter touch than you might expect from a seafood brand. The Our Story page says Fishwife's fish comes from Marine Stewardship Council or Aquaculture Stewardship Council certified fisheries, and it names six canneries across Washington State, Spain, Denmark, and Scotland. But Millstein told Marketing Brew in April 2022 that responsible sourcing was "table stakes these days," and that the main branding strategy was "messaging deliciousness, humor, exuberance, artfulness."

So the certifications sit on the website for the shopper who checks, while the tins and the posts sell taste. If every competitor in your category makes the same claim, put it in your proof, because it will not set you apart in a headline.

## Did Fishwife really grow without paid ads?

At first, yes, and this is the part of the story I think gets misread. [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2022/04/07/fishwife-made-its-presence-known-organically-now-it-s-thinking-bigger) reported in April 2022 that Fishwife had "not done any paid marketing." That line gets repeated as proof that great packaging is enough on its own.

The same article explains why it worked. Millstein credited much of the early growth to co-founder Caroline Goldfarb, saying Goldfarb's followers "helped things move really, really fast." The brand was also sending product to influencers and micro-influencers and hoping they would post. So the free reach had an engine behind it, an audience that existed on day one plus steady gifting, and Millstein said in that interview that the organic growth was "not gonna last forever."

While it lasted, that engine was strong. Millstein told Help Scout in 2023 that 40 percent of customers discover Fishwife through social media. The #tinnedfishdatenight hashtag had 7.4 million TikTok views by September 2022, [Bustle reported](https://www.bustle.com/life/tinned-fish-date-night-tiktok-trend), and Fishwife was one of the brands it named. Fishwife's own TikTok from May 2023 opens with "welcome to tinned fish tok," which shows the brand talking to a community that was already forming on the app.

<TikTok url="https://www.tiktok.com/@eatfishwife/video/7239035198522789162" title="Fishwife tinned fish tok TikTok, May 2023" />

The revenue Millstein disclosed on Shark Tank shows how fast that period moved. She left with $350,000 from Lori Greiner and Candace Nelson after countering their 10 percent offer with 6 percent plus advisory shares, [Tasting Table reported](https://www.tastingtable.com/1492483/what-to-know-about-fishwife-shark-tank) in January 2024.

The lesson I take from this is narrower than "skip ads." No paid marketing worked for a brand that launched with a built-in audience and a gifting habit. If you launch without a co-founder's following, budget for gifting or paid reach from the start instead of hoping the packaging travels alone. Duolingo is a useful contrast, because it built reach on one consistent social voice, as I explain in the [Duolingo breakdown](/blog/duolingo-social-media-strategy).

## Why collaborations drive Fishwife's marketing strategy

Millstein's own plan pointed away from big ad budgets. In April 2022, she told Marketing Brew she was "much less interested in just shoveling $10,000 into Facebook ads." By January 2025, she told [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.com.au/sectors/fishwifes-becca-millstein-talks-brand-journey-social-media-and-subscriptions-202501) that Fishwife runs collaborations "several a month" across food, fashion, and jewelry.

This is my central read of Fishwife: the collab calendar does the job a media budget does at most brands. Each partner brings its own audience and puts the tin somewhere new, whether that is a chili crisp fan's pantry, a sandal display, or a salad bowl. Because these collabs are physical things people can hold, they get posted the same way the tins do, so each one keeps paying out after launch day.

The Fly By Jing tin shows how a limited collab can turn into a lasting product. It launched in March 2022, Fishwife's [product page](https://eatfishwife.com/products/smoked-salmon-with-flybyjing-sichuan-chili-crisp) now calls it a "best-selling smash collaboration," and the partnership has since added a second flavor, [Smoked Salmon with Sweet & Spicy Zhong](https://eatfishwife.com/products/the-fly-by-jing-zhong-smoked-salmon-trio-3-pack), built on another Fly By Jing sauce. One collab became a product line.

The Sweetgreen collab went further and made the tin part of the meal. Sweetgreen's [August 2026 release](https://s28.q4cdn.com/367108596/files/doc_news/Sweetgreen-and-Fishwife-Bring-Tinned-Fish-to-the-Menu-for-the-First-Time-with-Summer-Nioise-2026.pdf) says the Summer Niçoise came with a custom co-branded tin of Fishwife Albacore Tuna with Spanish Lemon for guests to open themselves, and that it was the first time Fishwife appeared on a national restaurant menu. That put the brand in front of salad-chain customers who may never walk down the tinned fish aisle. Fishwife's [recipe page](https://eatfishwife.com/blogs/recipes/the-sweetgreen-x-fishwife-tuna-nicoise-salad) later noted that the limited-time collaboration sold out at Sweetgreen.

The newest collabs push the tin into categories that have nothing to do with fish. In October 2026, Fishwife's shop lists [Dieux x Fishwife eye masks](https://eatfishwife.com/products/dieux-x-fishwife-good-taste-forever-eye-masks) packed in collectible tins with Miller's artwork, and a [Bag Balm x Fishwife moisturizer](https://eatfishwife.com/products/bag-balm-x-fishwife-original-skin-moisturizer-tin) in a sardine-style tin, sold only in the brand's 12 Days of Fishwife advent calendar. The package that won the first look is now the thing partners want to borrow, which is why I'd plan collaborations as a calendar with a reach goal for each one, instead of treating them as one-off favors.

## Retail followed the attention

Stores came after the audience, and the timeline shows it. Fishwife announced a national expansion into select Whole Foods stores in April 2024, with two exclusive albacore tuna products, after conversations that began in 2021, The Metal Packager reported. In the same announcement, Millstein said the business grew nearly 180 percent year over year, against that 1 percent for the conventional category. Fishwife was in 1,800 retail locations that month, per [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/12/fishwife-founder-becca-millstein-tinned-fish), and in 5,000 brick-and-mortar retailers by January 2025, per Inside Retail.

In January 2026, [Sporked reported](https://sporked.com/article/fishwife-tinned-fish-costco-news/) a national Costco rollout, with a 3-pack of Albacore in Spicy Olive Oil at about $15, while 3-packs on Fishwife's own site go for over $30. A brand that grew on giftable, premium tins now also sells warehouse packs at about half its online price, which suggests it is reaching shoppers who never saw the TikToks.

## What to take from Fishwife

1. **Design for the photo before you buy reach.** Fishwife hired its illustrator before it filled a sample box, and the tin has worked as its launch ad ever since.
2. **Count the audience you start with.** "No paid ads" worked because a co-founder's following and steady gifting were already in place. Without that, budget for reach from day one.
3. **Say the plain thing on the label.** Literal product names keep the shopper oriented, so the personality can live in the name and the art.
4. **Run collaborations as a calendar.** Several a month, each in a new category, with a partner who brings an audience you do not have yet.
5. **Make your package the collab.** The Sweetgreen tin, the Dieux eye masks, and the Bag Balm tin all borrow Fishwife's format, which means the brand shows up even when the product is not fish.

## The catch: you have to earn the collab calendar

The easy read is that any small brand can run Fishwife's play by lining up partners. I don't think that holds, because every big collab arrived after Fishwife already had attention. The Fly By Jing tin launched in March 2022, the same spring Marketing Brew described growth powered by Goldfarb's following. Sweetgreen came in 2026, after the brand had passed 5,000 stores, and its co-founder Nicolas Jammet said in the release that "Fishwife has built an exciting brand around exceptional tinned seafood, bold flavor and thoughtful sourcing." Partners were buying into a brand that already had a look and a crowd.

There is a second tension in the Costco move. Collabs keep a brand feeling special, and warehouse packs at half the online price pull the other way. I'm watching whether the collab calendar can hold Fishwife's premium feel while Costco brings in volume.

So the order matters more than the tactic. At Green House, the clients I work with are small and mid-size local businesses, and the version I'd test first with them is small: pick two nearby brands whose customers look like yours, make one limited product or event together, and track how many new followers or email sign-ups each partner sends your way. If one collab brings in more new people than a month of regular posting, that is the first entry on your calendar.
