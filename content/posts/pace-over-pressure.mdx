In May 2026 I spent three weeks in Italy on the Utah Valley University (UVU) faculty-led entrepreneurship study abroad, Rome, Florence, Milan, Venice, visiting local entrepreneurs, a Venetian glass manufacturer, and Milan University, comparing artisan business models with modern startup ecosystems. It was a business course. The lesson I brought home wasn't on the syllabus.

## The furnace

On Murano we stood in a workshop while a glassblower worked. No rush. No shortcuts. He turned the rod, checked the shape, returned it to the heat, turned it again. The tourists around him had crossed the world to watch this, and he moved like the audience wasn't there.

At the time I was carrying the pressure most marketing students carry: be the best at everything by graduation, have the portfolio, have the numbers, have the job. Watching him, the pressure looked ridiculous. He wasn't trying to be the best glassblower today. He was making this piece well, and then the next one.

## What it changed

It changed how I approach the work I actually want to do, creating moments people want to be part of. A launch, a pop-up, a campaign: none of them are built in a panic. They're built the way he built that piece. Shape, heat, check, again.

It also changed how I read other brands. The launches that feel like moments are almost always the ones where somebody clearly took their time on the details, the product drop where the packaging matched the pop-up matched the caption. Pace shows.

## What I'm doing about it

- **One post a week on this site**, done properly, instead of ten rushed ones.
- **Shipping work I can stand behind** at Hope Scholars, Green House, and UVU Athletics, and saying so when it isn't ready.
- **Rereading this** when the pressure comes back, because it will.

You don't have to be the best today. You just have to keep improving.
