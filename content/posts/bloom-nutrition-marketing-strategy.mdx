Bloom Nutrition's creator program has drawn more than 12 billion views and takes about 75 percent of the marketing budget. No pop-up can compete with that on reach, so Bloom's events need a different job. The strongest one Bloom has run gave guests a reason to leave their name, and that is the standard every brand event should be held to: how many people walk out as contacts you can reach again.

**The short version**

- **The audience came before the product.** Co-founder Mari Llewellyn's followers were buying her guides and gear before Bloom launched in 2019.
- **Creators carry the reach.** About three quarters of the marketing budget goes to creators, and the program has drawn more than 12 billion views.
- **The best pop-up asked for something.** Bloom's 2025 Treat Shoppe added an on-site sign-up, and 5,500 people joined its TYB community in the first month.
- **Club Apple pushed the ask earlier.** The October 2026 SoHo pop-up handed out RSVPs through Instagram comments and DMs before the doors opened.

## What gap did Bloom Nutrition's founders see?

Bloom began as one person's Instagram account. In 2017, after nine months focused on fitness, Llewellyn posted a before-and-after photo to an account with 900 followers, at the urging of her now-husband, Greg LaVecchia, she wrote in an as-told-to essay for [Inc. in August 2024](https://www.inc.com/sydney-sladovnik/how-an-army-of-tiktokers-drove-12-billion-video-views-for-my-company.html). Within a year she had more than 400,000, and followers were messaging her for her meal plan. The couple sold a fitness guide as a $5 PDF, then added resistance bands in early 2018, and all 900 sold out in a few hours.

The product gap came from her own routine. Llewellyn was mixing protein powders and pre-workouts into her diet but felt most were made for men, and as someone who had struggled with anxiety, she didn't want "an aggressively caffeinated powder" every day. The couple moved into supplements in 2018 and launched Bloom in 2019, and by then, in her words, "my social media community already felt very much a part of the brand."

Bloom had buyers asking for products before it had products. That's the advantage most new brands try to buy later with ads.

## How did Bloom Greens become the hero product?

Bloom started with blends made with collagen, adaptogens, and prebiotics, then launched its Greens & Superfoods blend early in 2020, Llewellyn wrote. People were paying closer attention to nutrition during the pandemic just as TikTok was taking off, and in her words, "Greens sales took off."

The styling is why a greens powder could travel on a video app. Bloom's About Us page lists its principles as Flavor Focused, Function First, and Female Forward, and promises "delicious flavors you can't find anywhere else." Greens show up in a wavy glass with a strawberry, a kiwi slice, and a heart-shaped straw, closer to a mocktail than a health chore.

A drink that looks like a treat gives a creator a first frame worth posting, which matters more on TikTok than any ingredient list.

## What is Bloom Nutrition's marketing strategy?

Bloom builds reach on other people's audiences, on purpose. It direct-messaged wellness creators and set up an in-house influencer team separate from its social media team, and since 2021 that team has worked with 5,600 creators whose Bloom videos have drawn more than 12 billion views, Llewellyn told Inc. She credits the freedom those creators got: "It wasn't so much a strategy as it was letting influencers be themselves." The program costs about 75 percent of the marketing budget, and she tied it to nearly $170 million in revenue in 2023.

Bloom's founder was the creator, so a creator program simply scaled what already worked. And when three quarters of the budget already buys reach, every other channel, events included, has to earn its place another way.

## What happened at Bloom's NYC market pop-up?

Bloom ran a corner-market pop-up in New York in September 2023 and posted a 17-second recap from its @bloom TikTok account on September 9. The caption calls it a wrap on "our market pop-up this weekend in NYC," thanks "our community for showing up and showing out," and tags it #nyfw.

The edit moves through eight labeled cuts: a green storefront with a "Find your flavor" sign, a "Greens wall" of canisters stacked like produce, a cooler of stick packs, "Bloom branded fruit," a flavor quiz on a phone, custom bags and baskets, a strawberry-print SUV labeled "The Bloom Bronco," and finally groups of guests over the line "And our community ofc."

The set did most of the work. Every station was a shot a guest could take on their own phone, so the people in the room filmed the ad for Bloom. Saves outnumber comments 68 to 11, which suggests viewers bookmarked the clip rather than joining a conversation.

Two changes would make it stronger. The recap saves the community for the last cut, so it reads as a store tour, and a people-first edit is worth testing against it. At Yoodlize, I ran A/B tests on YouTube ads before launch, and the winning version brought people to the site at 31 percent lower cost, so a quick test like that settles the question with data instead of opinion. The recap also ends with nowhere to go. A simple prompt like "tag yourself if you came" would have turned views into names.

## What did the Treat Shoppe pop-up do differently?

Two years later, Bloom tried the opposite at the Treat Shoppe, a SoHo pop-up in September 2025. It launched its community on TYB, a fan rewards app, the same week and set up an on-site sign-up booth so guests could join before they left. TYB's [case study](https://www.tyb.xyz/case-studies/bloom-activates-community-at-exclusive-irl-experiences-driving-68-growth-during-treat-shoppe-week) reports more than 250 RSVPs, 5,500 people joining Bloom's TYB community in the first month, and 13,000 new email subscribers.

Bloom put the list to work right away. Screenshots in the case study show it asking members for their favorite Treat Shoppe moment, which drew 638 responses, and then where it should pop up next, which drew 1,986. Jillian Reulein, Bloom's head of social and content, said the launch let the team "capture that initial excitement and turn an in-person moment into an ongoing relationship."

Read the numbers with care: TYB is a vendor reporting on its own client, and its claim that members order 73 percent more often compares people who chose to join with people who didn't. The structure is still the lesson. Guests got a reason and a place to leave their name, and Bloom used the list within days.

## Can a pop-up compete with Bloom's creators on reach?

Not at Bloom's size. The 2023 recap has 26,200 views, while the creator program has drawn more than 12 billion, so even a well-built set barely registers next to the channel Bloom already pays for.

Bloom's leadership doesn't plan events around impressions either. In April 2026, Bloom wrapped a 7-Eleven near the Palm Springs airport in pink for Coachella weekend, with DJs, samples, and an airbrush merch station, to promote its energy drinks' nationwide launch at 7-Eleven, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/bloom-pacsun-and-more-chase-coachella-crowds-on-the-way-to-the-show-with-roadside-activations/). CEO Greg LaVecchia said an event is easy to approve when it makes sense both digitally and in real life, and that the team isn't "reverse engineering the whole program for how many impressions we're going to get at what cost."

Modern Retail also reported that Bloom would measure the takeover less by foot traffic and financial return than by awareness, social chatter, and virality, and that it was the second of five or six large activations planned for 2026. That scorecard misses what an event uniquely offers. Chatter is what Bloom already buys at scale from creators. A pop-up is the one place a fan stands in front of the brand with no platform in between, and the metric that captures that is owned contacts. A creator's audience is rented. A guest list is owned.

## How did Bloom run its Club Apple pop-up?

Club Apple ran for two days in October 2026. Bloom's [event page](https://bloomnu.com/pages/club-apple-event-new-york-city) set it at 9 W 8th St in Manhattan on October 24 and 25, with Crisp Apple drinks dressed with toppings, a portrait studio guided by editorial photographers, mystery gifts, and a request to come dressed in red. The [NYC for FREE listing](https://www.nycforfree.co/events/bloom-nutrition-club-apple) added unreleased flavor tastings, DJ sets, and a vote on which unreleased flavor should launch next.

This time the capture started before the doors opened. On October 20, Bloom's Instagram announced Club Apple and asked people to comment "Club Apple" to get an official RSVP in their DMs, and the post had 1,966 comments by October 24. A teaser on October 23 asked followers to tag a plus-one, and the event page's RSVP buttons led to an RSVP page on Rumor, an event app.

<Instagram url="https://www.instagram.com/p/DeuT56-IJlp/" title="Bloom Nutrition announces Club Apple on Instagram, October 2026" />

On opening day, Bloom's TikTok posted the hours and address with a "Come say hi" clip, and a day earlier it promoted a [TikTok Shop LIVE from Club Apple](https://www.tiktok.com/@bloom/video/7699976409422335245) with Greg and Mari as guests. That stacked three asks on one weekend: comment for a spot, show up in person, and buy on a live stream.

The flavor vote was the most valuable piece, because how it was collected, through a sign-up or a sticker wall, decided whether Bloom left SoHo with a list of its own or a stack of Instagram comments.

## What to take from Bloom Nutrition's marketing strategy

1. **Build the audience before the product.** Bloom sold guides and resistance bands to Llewellyn's followers first, so its launch had buyers waiting.
2. **Make the product easy to film.** A greens drink with a strawberry and a heart-shaped straw gives creators a first frame worth posting.
3. **Let creators carry reach, and let them be themselves.** With about 75 percent of the budget there, the rest of the plan doesn't have to chase views.
4. **Build the set as a backdrop.** Every station at the 2023 market was a shot a guest would take on their own phone. For more crowd-first examples, see the [best brand activations of 2026](/blog/10-brand-moments-people-showed-up-for-2026).
5. **Ask for something before the doors open.** Club Apple's comment-for-an-RSVP post turned a launch announcement into nearly 2,000 comments.
6. **Collect contacts on site, then use them.** The Treat Shoppe's sign-up booth gave Bloom a list it went back to within days.

## The catch: most of Bloom's list still lives on Instagram

Club Apple looks like Bloom solving the pop-up problem, but it isn't solved yet. A comment and a DM thread are contact on a platform Bloom rents, the same way it rents its creators' audiences, so the RSVP push captured attention without guaranteeing an email address or phone number Bloom controls. The Rumor page may have given Bloom more, but nothing public says what it collected. The Treat Shoppe is still the only Bloom event with a reported owned list, and that report comes from the vendor that sold the tool.

LaVecchia told Modern Retail that Bloom wants to be associated with "tentpole moments" through the year, and with three quarters of the budget in creators, a pop-up can look like a tentpole that doesn't need counting. It should be counted anyway, because events are the one channel where a brand meets its fans directly.

If you run an event this season, track two numbers before you look at views: how many guests gave you a way to reach them that you own, and how many answered the first time you did.
