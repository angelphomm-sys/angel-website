In 2017, Mari Llewellyn posted a before-and-after photo to an Instagram account with 900 followers, and within a year she had more than 400,000. Bloom Nutrition's marketing strategy has run on audiences ever since, first hers and then those of 5,600 creators. That program already pays for reach, so Bloom's pop-ups need a different job, and I think that job is capture: turning the people who show up into contacts Bloom can reach again.

**The short version**

- **The audience came before the product.** Llewellyn's followers were buying her guides and gear before Bloom launched in 2019.
- **Creators carry the reach.** Bloom's creator program takes about 75 percent of its marketing budget and has drawn more than 12 billion views.
- **The best pop-up asked for something.** Bloom's 2025 Treat Shoppe added an on-site sign-up, and 5,500 people joined its TYB community in the first month.
- **Club Apple ran the play in October 2026.** Bloom's two-day SoHo pop-up handed out RSVPs through Instagram comments and DMs before the doors opened.

## What gap did Bloom Nutrition's founders see?

Llewellyn told the story in an as-told-to essay for [Inc. in August 2024](https://www.inc.com/sydney-sladovnik/how-an-army-of-tiktokers-drove-12-billion-video-views-for-my-company.html). After she spent nine months on fitness, her now-husband, Greg LaVecchia, encouraged her to post the before-and-after photo, and followers soon messaged her for her meal plan. The couple sold a fitness guide as a $5 PDF, then added resistance bands in early 2018, and all 900 sold out in a few hours.

The product gap came from her own routine. Llewellyn was mixing protein powders and pre-workouts into her diet but felt most of them were made for men, and as someone who had struggled with anxiety, she did not want "an aggressively caffeinated powder" every day. The couple pivoted to supplements in 2018 and launched Bloom in 2019, and by then, in her words, "my social media community already felt very much a part of the brand."

That order shapes everything after it. Bloom had people asking for products before it had products, so the brand was built in front of an audience that already trusted its founder.

## How did Bloom Greens become the hero product?

Bloom started with blends made with collagen, adaptogens, and prebiotics, then launched its Greens & Superfoods blend early in 2020, Llewellyn wrote in Inc. People were paying more attention to nutrition during the pandemic while TikTok was growing fast, and in her words, "Greens sales took off."

The styling explains why a greens powder could travel on a video app. Bloom's About Us page lists its principles as Flavor Focused, Function First, and Female Forward, and the flavor one promises "delicious flavors you can't find anywhere else." Greens arrive in a wavy glass with a strawberry, a kiwi slice, and a heart-shaped straw, which looks closer to a mocktail than a health chore.

The product was built to be filmed, and a drink that looks like a treat gives a creator something to show in the first second.

## What is Bloom Nutrition's marketing strategy?

Bloom borrowed other creators' audiences on purpose. It direct-messaged wellness creators and set up an in-house influencer team, separate from its social media team, and since 2021 that team has worked with 5,600 creators whose Bloom-related videos have drawn more than 12 billion views, Llewellyn told Inc. She credits the creative freedom those creators got: "It wasn't so much a strategy as it was letting influencers be themselves." The program costs about 75 percent of Bloom's marketing budget, and she tied it to nearly $170 million in revenue in 2023.

[Graza](/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza) started from the other side, seeding food creators with free oil and asking for no posts. Bloom's founder was the creator, so a creator program extended what already worked. When three quarters of the budget already buys reach, every other channel, events included, has to earn its place some other way.

## What happened at Bloom's NYC market pop-up?

Bloom ran a corner-market pop-up in New York in September 2023 and posted a 17-second recap from its @bloom TikTok account on September 9. The caption calls it a wrap on "our market pop-up this weekend in NYC," thanks "our community for showing up and showing out," and tags it #nyfw.

<TikTok url="https://www.tiktok.com/@bloom/video/7276965640181763371" title="Bloom Nutrition NYC market pop-up recap on TikTok, September 2023" />

The edit moves through eight labeled cuts: a green storefront with a "Find your flavor" sign, a "Greens wall" of canisters stacked like produce, a cooler of stick packs, "Bloom branded fruit," a flavor quiz on a phone, custom bags and baskets, a strawberry-print SUV labeled "The Bloom Bronco," and finally groups of guests over the line "And our community ofc."

I checked the public counts on October 24, 2026.

The set does most of the work. Every station is a shot a guest could take on their own phone, so the people in the room film the ad for Bloom. Saves outnumber comments 68 to 11, which suggests viewers filed the clip away instead of joining a conversation.

I would change two things. The captions save the community for the last cut, so the recap reads as a store tour, and I would test a people-first cut against it. At Yoodlize, I split-tested two YouTube thumbnails, a party-first one against an app-first one, and let click-through pick the winner, and the same setup works here. I would also give viewers a next step, such as "tag yourself if you came," because the recap ends with nowhere to go. As event content, it works, but it leaves Bloom with views and nothing it can reach again.

## What did the Treat Shoppe pop-up do differently?

Two years later, Bloom tried the opposite at the Treat Shoppe, a SoHo pop-up in September 2025. It launched its community on TYB, a fan rewards app, the same week and set up an on-site sign-up booth so guests could join before they left. TYB's [case study](https://www.tyb.xyz/case-studies/bloom-activates-community-at-exclusive-irl-experiences-driving-68-growth-during-treat-shoppe-week) reports more than 250 RSVPs to the event, 5,500 people joining Bloom's TYB community in the first month, and 13,000 new email subscribers.

The list paid off after the event, too. Screenshots in the case study show Bloom asking members for their favorite Treat Shoppe moment, which drew 638 responses, and then asking where it should pop up next, which drew 1,986. Jillian Reulein, Bloom's head of social and content, said in the case study that the launch let the team "capture that initial excitement and turn an in-person moment into an ongoing relationship." That is the job description I would give every Bloom pop-up.

TYB is a vendor reporting on its own client, and its claim that members order 73 percent more often compares people who chose to join with people who did not. The structure is still the lesson: guests got a reason and a place to leave their name, and Bloom used the list within days.

## Can a pop-up compete with Bloom's creators on reach?

At Bloom's size, it cannot, and that is the point I want to argue. The recap has 26,200 views, while the creator program has drawn more than 12 billion, so even a well-built set barely registers next to the channel Bloom already pays for.

Bloom's leadership does not plan events around impressions either. In April 2026, Bloom wrapped a 7-Eleven near the Palm Springs airport in pink for Coachella weekend, with DJs, samples, and an airbrush merch station, to promote its energy drinks' nationwide launch at 7-Eleven, [Modern Retail reported](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/bloom-pacsun-and-more-chase-coachella-crowds-on-the-way-to-the-show-with-roadside-activations/) that month. CEO Greg LaVecchia said an event is easy to approve when it makes sense both digitally and in real life, and that the team is not "reverse engineering the whole program for how many impressions we're going to get at what cost."

Modern Retail also reported that Bloom would measure the takeover less by foot traffic and financial return than by awareness, social chatter, and virality, and that it was the second of five or six large activations planned for 2026. That scorecard is where I disagree. Chatter is what Bloom already buys at scale from creators, while a pop-up offers something no feed can: a person standing in front of you. I would score Bloom's events on how many of those people leave as contacts Bloom can reach again, because a creator's audience is rented and a guest list is owned.

## How did Bloom run its Club Apple pop-up?

Club Apple ran for two days in October 2026. Bloom's [event page](https://bloomnu.com/pages/club-apple-event-new-york-city) sets it at 9 W 8th St in Manhattan on October 24 and 25, 2026, with Crisp Apple drinks dressed with toppings, a portrait studio guided by editorial photographers, mystery gifts, and a request to come dressed in red. The [NYC for FREE listing](https://www.nycforfree.co/events/bloom-nutrition-club-apple) adds unreleased flavor tastings, DJ sets, and a vote on which unreleased flavor should launch next.

This time the capture started before the doors opened. On October 20, Bloom's Instagram announced Club Apple and asked people to comment "Club Apple" to get an official RSVP in their DMs, and that post had 1,966 comments when I checked on October 24. A teaser on October 23 asked followers to tag a plus-one for an invite, and the event page's RSVP and "Get on the list" buttons both lead to an RSVP page on Rumor, an event app.

On opening day, Bloom's TikTok posted the hours and address with a "Come say hi" clip, and a day earlier it promoted a [TikTok Shop LIVE from Club Apple](https://www.tiktok.com/@bloom/video/7699976409422335245) with Greg and Mari as guests. That stacks three asks on one weekend: comment for a spot, show up in person, and buy on a live stream.

The flavor vote is the piece I am watching, because whether it runs through a sign-up or a sticker wall decides if Bloom leaves SoHo with a list of its own or only a stack of Instagram comments.

## What to take from Bloom Nutrition's marketing strategy

1. **Build the audience before the product.** Bloom sold guides and resistance bands to Llewellyn's followers first, so its launch had buyers waiting.
2. **Make the product easy to film.** A greens drink with a strawberry and a heart-shaped straw gives creators a first frame worth posting.
3. **Pay creators for reach, and let them be themselves.** With about 75 percent of the budget there, the rest of the plan does not have to chase views.
4. **Build the set as a backdrop.** Every station at the 2023 market, like the Greens wall, was a shot a guest would take on their own phone. For more crowd-first examples, see the [best brand activations of 2026](/blog/10-brand-moments-people-showed-up-for-2026).
5. **Ask for something before the doors open.** Club Apple's comment-for-an-RSVP post turned a launch announcement into nearly 2,000 comments.
6. **Collect contacts on site, then use them.** The Treat Shoppe's sign-up booth gave Bloom a list it went back to with questions within days.

## The catch: most of Bloom's list still lives on Instagram

The easy read of Club Apple is that Bloom has solved the pop-up problem, and I don't think it has yet. A comment and a DM thread are contact on a platform Bloom rents, the same way it rents its creators' audiences, so the RSVP push captures attention without guaranteeing an email address or a phone number Bloom controls. The Rumor RSVP page may hand Bloom more, but nothing public says what it collects. The Treat Shoppe is the only Bloom event here with a reported owned list, and that report comes from the vendor that sold the tool.

LaVecchia told Modern Retail that Bloom wants to be associated with "tentpole moments" through the year, and with three quarters of the budget in creators, a pop-up may simply be a tentpole Bloom does not need to count. I would still count it, because events are the one place a brand meets its fans without a platform in the middle.

If you run an event this season, track two numbers before you look at views: how many guests gave you a way to reach them that you own, and how many answered the first time you did. I'll be watching Bloom's Club Apple recap for both.
