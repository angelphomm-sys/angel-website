Prime Collectives was my photography business: seniors, couples, families, headshots, the occasional proposal on a beach at golden hour. I started it in 2018 and ran it until August 2026, managing the whole client lifecycle, booking, shoot planning, the shoot, editing and retouching in Lightroom, delivery, and growing it almost entirely through referrals and Instagram.

It looks like a side business. It was a marketing education.

## You are selling a feeling, then a file

Nobody books a photographer for the JPEGs. They book the way they'll feel looking at them in ten years. Every shoot starts with that question, what does this person want to feel, and the posing, the light, the location follow from it. It's the same question a beauty brand asks before a launch. The product is the shortcut to the feeling.

## Consistency is the brand

Clients found me on Instagram and hired me because the feed looked like one person made it: the same warm edit, the same unposed energy, the same kind of light. A recognizable style is a promise. I learned to protect it, to turn down work that didn't fit, and to edit the last photo in a gallery to match the first.

## Referrals are a content strategy

The best marketing was a client posting the photos. So delivery was designed for it: a few edited previews within 24 hours so they'd post while the excitement was high, a full gallery a week later, and a gentle ask to tag the account. Most of my bookings came from someone seeing a friend's photos.

## The moment is usually one second long

A proposal happens once. The ring shot, the reaction, the laugh on the bike, you get the frame or you don't. Eight years of that trained the reflex I now use at events: know where the moment is going to happen, be there early, and don't look away.

## What it means for the work I want

I'm aiming at social, content, and events roles for consumer, sports, and entertainment brands. Those jobs need someone who can see the shot, direct it, and get it delivered, and who understands that every post is a piece of a brand someone will judge in a half-second scroll. That's the whole business, in one line.
