Graza's marketing strategy usually gets credited to its green squeeze bottle, and it did get the oil noticed at its January 2022 launch. What built the business was the order of the moves around it: free oil for creators, a sellout online, then grocery shelves, with its largest paid campaign saved for 2026. That order is the part you can copy.

**The short version**

- **The bottle was the hook.** It was easy to film and just as easy for competitors to copy.
- **Seeding came before selling.** Creators got free oil weeks before launch, with no strings.
- **Retail came after proof.** Whole Foods came after an online sellout, and its largest paid campaign came four years later.
- **The names carry the brand now.** Drizzle and Sizzle tell you the job, which no copied bottle can take.

Graza's own TikTok from June 2023 runs the caption "so fresh, squeezy, and clean." Notice that it sells the squeeze itself and never mentions the olives.

## How did Graza become popular so fast?

Graza launched on its own website on January 11, 2022, with an audience already waiting. Starting about three weeks before launch, the team mailed roughly 25 packs of oil a week to food creators with no posting requirement, per a [2023 Klaviyo case study](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/guerrilla-marketing-for-ecommerce). [Marketing Brew reported in 2022](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2022/02/17/inside-the-social-strategy-that-squeezy-bottle-olive-oil-brand-graza-used-to-make-its-dtc-debut) that more than 100 packages went out before the site went live, with smaller creators getting oil weeks early and the biggest accounts closer to launch day.

The list was picked for curiosity more than reach. Social media consultant Kendall Dickieson told Marketing Brew, "We went after people that if you saw this on their account, people would want to ask about it." Graza also sent samples to 80 retailers, so shop owners got the same early, no-strings treatment as creators. Bloom Nutrition grew from the opposite direction, with a founder who was already the creator, which I cover in [Bloom's community playbook](/blog/bloom-nutrition-marketing-strategy).

The payoff came in launch week. Cookbook author Molly Baz ran out of her gifted oil, Graza sent her a refill, and she posted it. "The second she posted that on her story, it was a bloodbath," co-founder Andrew Benin told Klaviyo. Graza sold out in its first week with $100,000 in revenue and passed $500,000 by April, which means the early growth came from viewers spotting an unfamiliar bottle in a creator's kitchen and asking about it. That only happens if you put the product there first.

## Why did Graza put olive oil in a squeeze bottle?

The squeeze bottle gave Graza something no other olive oil had on day one. Benin told [FoodNavigator in 2022](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2022/01/12/Graza-debuts-premium-affordable-olive-oil-in-squeeze-bottle-format/) that it was "the first squeeze bottle brought to market in our category. We own the molds."

His stated bet was about how often people would reach for it. He told [AIGA's Eye on Design in 2022](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/how-to-build-an-entire-brand-identity-around-a-squeeze-bottle/) that the team wanted to raise "propensity of use because the form factor kind of does the work for us." I read that as a repeat-purchase plan: a bottle you can squeeze one-handed over a pan gets used more often, and oil that runs out sooner gets reordered sooner.

There is a second reason Benin didn't spell out. On a phone screen, a glass bottle glugs while a squeeze bottle draws a clean line, so every creator video showed the product doing its job. Graza's pitch to creators, per Marketing Brew, was a single question: "How much easier would life be if olive oil came in a squeezy bottle?" Fishwife made a similar packaging-first bet with its tins, which I broke down in the [Fishwife post](/blog/fishwife-marketing-strategy).

So the bottle made the oil easier to use at home and easier to show on camera, and that pairing is why it got noticed so quickly.

## Drizzle and Sizzle do the explaining

Graza launched with two oils and put the job in each name. Drizzle is a 500 ml finishing oil and Sizzle is a 750 ml cooking oil, per AIGA, and they launched at $20 and $15, per FoodNavigator. Benin described Sizzle as "your everyday cooking oil for breakfast, lunch, and dinner" and called Graza "a non-heritage, non-luxury company."

That positioning needed plain words. [Tasting Table's 2026 explainer](https://www.tastingtable.com/2194996/why-graza-olive-oil-popular/) places Graza in the gap between cheap supermarket oil and premium brands that often cost $30 or more. The names explain that gap without a lecture, because you don't need to know olive varieties to choose between cooking and finishing.

The hand-drawn labels by Gander, a New York agency, keep the tone casual. By March 2025 the line had a third oil, Frizzle, and Graza's TikTok sold all three as "The Trio," with the three names as its hashtags.

That makes the names the asset with the longest shelf life. A competitor can order its own squeeze bottle molds, but borrowing names like these would read as a copy on sight.

## Graza ran the grocery launch in reverse

The usual grocery path starts with slotting fees, payments to a retailer for shelf space before any shopper has tried the product. The [FTC's 2003 staff report on slotting allowances](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2003/11/ftc-releases-grocery-industry-slotting-allowance-report) found that slotting fees "can make up a large fraction of the revenues earned by some products in their first year."

Graza let the shelf come to it. After the online sellout, a Whole Foods representative reached out, and by the end of that year Graza's bottles were on Whole Foods shelves nationwide, [Inside Retail Asia reported in 2025](https://insideretail.asia/2025/12/16/how-graza-became-a-48-million-olive-oil-brand-stocked-by-whole-foods-and-target/). The same story estimated 2024 revenue at $48.4 million.

So the expensive steps came after Graza had sales to show for the cheap ones, which is the main thing a small launch budget can learn from it.

## Where the squeeze bottle stopped being an advantage

This is where I part ways with the usual read. Most of the top-ranking Graza breakdowns credit the bottle, but it is the one piece of the strategy that stopped being Graza's alone.

Competitors copied it first. California Olive Ranch launched a 500 ml squeezable "Chef's Bottle" in March 2024 after customers called asking for one, [Modern Retail reported in April 2024](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/to-squeeze-or-not-to-squeeze-the-olive-oil-world-is-divided-over-plastic-packaging/), and Benin had earlier accused Brightland of copying the design on LinkedIn before apologizing. The same story quoted Kosterina founder Katina Mountanos: "Olive oil should never be packaged in plastic."

Then Graza moved past the bottle itself. In August 2025 it began selling Sizzle and Drizzle in glass, and Benin told [The Dieline](https://thedieline.com/ex-squeeze-me-did-graza-just-go-glass/) there had been "clear feedback from some people who simply prefer their Extra Virgin Olive Oil in glass." Costs pressed on the model too: [Modern Retail reported in October 2023](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/how-brands-like-graza-are-dealing-with-olive-oil-price-hikes/) that Graza was paying more than double its early 2022 import costs and had raised its price from $15 to $16 a bottle.

At 28,000 locations, most shoppers meet Graza on a shelf near other squeeze bottles, so the name and label do more of the selling than the shape. That is why I think the 2026 mayo launch is the real test. If people buy Graza mayo, the brand is the names and the voice, and the bottle was only ever the hook. I'd also have offered glass as a choice in year two, before critics could frame it as a concession.

The lesson from Graza's fourth year is that a packaging hook lasts about as long as it takes a competitor to order molds, and the names kept working after that.

## How to use Graza's playbook on your own launch

- **Design the on-camera moment first.** Decide what the product does in a 10-second video, then design the package around it.
- **Seed for curiosity.** Send product to creators whose followers would ask about it, and attach no posting requirement.
- **Prove demand online, then pitch retail.** A sold-out first week is a stronger pitch to a buyer than a forecast.
- **Name the job.** A name that says what the product does can stretch to a new product line.

If you're launching something this year, test the hook and the name separately. Post the product with no logo in frame and see whether people ask what it is, then put the name on a plain package and see whether people know what it's for. Graza needed both, and only one of them got harder to copy with time.
