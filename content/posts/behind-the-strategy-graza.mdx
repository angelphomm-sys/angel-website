A few years before Graza existed, Andrew Benin borrowed his mother-in-law's Opel Corsa in southern Spain and drove four hours through olive groves from Córdoba to Jaén, a trip he describes on [Graza's own blog](https://www.graza.co/blogs/culture/casual-chats-with-andrew-benin). Graza's marketing strategy usually gets credited to the green squeeze bottle that came out of that obsession, and the bottle did get the oil noticed at its January 2022 launch. What built the business was the order of the moves around it: free oil for creators, a sellout online, then grocery shelves, with its largest paid campaign saved for 2026.

**The short version**

- **The idea came from an objection.** A chef told Benin the world needed good olive oil for everyone, which set the price and the everyday tone.
- **Seeding came before selling.** Creators and shop owners got free oil weeks before launch, with no posting requirement.
- **Retail came after proof.** Whole Foods reached out after an online sellout, and the largest paid campaign came four years later.
- **The names carry the brand now.** Drizzle and Sizzle tell you the job, and a competitor can copy a bottle far more easily than a name.

## Where did the idea for Graza come from?

Benin was living with his wife's family in Cádiz and was between jobs when he took that drive, he wrote on Graza's blog. He came back with so much oil that the family held a 10-oil tasting in their dining room, and he flew home to Brooklyn with cases of it in his checked luggage.

His first stop was Gramercy Tavern, where he poured the oils for executive chef Michael Anthony. Anthony pushed back on the idea of another small-batch, expensive European import. As Benin told [FoodNavigator in 2022](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2022/01/12/Graza-debuts-premium-affordable-olive-oil-in-squeeze-bottle-format/), Anthony's response was, "I really think what we need is this product, but for everyone." Benin and his co-founder, COO Allen Dushi, built the brand around a single-origin Picual oil from one supplier in Andalusia, per the same story.

The gap Anthony pointed to was real. [Tasting Table's 2026 explainer](https://www.tastingtable.com/2194996/why-graza-olive-oil-popular/) describes supermarket olive oil as split between bottom-shelf bottles priced in the low teens or single digits and better brands that can run $30 or more, with Graza aimed at the middle. That middle position is the brief behind everything that follows: an oil good enough for a chef and priced and packaged for a weeknight.

## Why did Graza put olive oil in a squeeze bottle?

The squeeze bottle gave Graza something no other olive oil had on day one. Benin told FoodNavigator that it was "the first squeeze bottle brought to market in our category. We own the molds."

His stated bet was about how often people would reach for it. He told [AIGA's Eye on Design in 2022](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/how-to-build-an-entire-brand-identity-around-a-squeeze-bottle/) that the team wanted to raise "propensity of use because the form factor kind of does the work for us." I read that as a repeat-purchase plan: a bottle you can squeeze one-handed over a pan gets used more often, and oil that runs out sooner gets reordered sooner.

<Figure src="/images/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza/graza-sizzle-drizzle-squeeze-bottles.jpg" alt="A tall dark green Sizzle squeeze bottle with a yellow label beside a shorter Drizzle bottle with a light green label, both with pointed nozzle caps" caption="Sizzle and Drizzle share one bottle shape and nozzle, so the label color and the name do the sorting. Image: Graza." ratio="1/1" />

There is a second reason Benin didn't spell out. On a phone screen, a glass bottle glugs while a squeeze bottle draws a clean line, so every creator video showed the product doing its job. Graza's pitch to creators, per Marketing Brew, was a single question: "How much easier would life be if olive oil came in a squeezy bottle?" Fishwife made a similar packaging-first bet with its tins, which I broke down in the [Fishwife post](/blog/fishwife-marketing-strategy).

Graza's own TikTok from June 2023 runs the caption "so fresh, squeezy, and clean." Notice that it sells the squeeze itself and never mentions the olives.

<TikTok url="https://www.tiktok.com/@getgraza/video/7247166475998563630" title="Graza squeeze bottle TikTok, June 2023" />

So the bottle made the oil easier to use at home and easier to show on camera, and that pairing is why it got noticed so quickly.

## How Graza's branding makes olive oil feel everyday

The bottle got attention, but the look and the names told people what kind of oil this was. Graza hired Gander, a New York agency, to build the identity, and Gander partner Katie Levy told AIGA that the type-led label options were "beautiful, but missing the levity at the core of the brand." So the labels went to hand-drawn illustrations: an olive with a spigot on Drizzle and a woman in a polka-dot dress squeezing oil into a pan on Sizzle. Levy said the drawings started as sketches "drawn with a Sharpie on a Post-It" and that the oil was meant to feel "very everyday," not saved for special occasions.

<Figure src="/images/blog/behind-the-strategy-graza/graza-illustration-share-card.jpg" alt="Dark green line drawings of cooks, olives, frying pans, and an olive tree on a lime background around the words Always fresh, never blended, graza.co" caption="This illustration card is the image graza.co shows when someone shares the site. Notice that every figure is doing an ordinary kitchen task, which is the everyday tone Gander was after. Image: Graza." ratio="64/35" />

The names do the same job in words. Graza launched with two oils and put the use in each name: Drizzle is a 500 ml finishing oil and Sizzle is a 750 ml cooking oil, per AIGA. FoodNavigator listed launch prices of $20 for the 500 ml bottle and $15 for the 750 ml bottle. Benin described Sizzle as "your everyday cooking oil for breakfast, lunch, and dinner" and called Graza "a non-heritage, non-luxury company."

That positioning needed plain words, because you don't need to know olive varieties to choose between cooking and finishing. By March 2025 the line had a third oil, Frizzle, for high-heat cooking, and Graza's TikTok sold all three as "The Trio," with the three names as its hashtags.

<TikTok url="https://www.tiktok.com/@getgraza/video/7478107694751337774" title="Graza The Trio TikTok, March 2025" />

That makes the names the asset with the longest shelf life. A competitor can order its own squeeze bottle molds, but borrowing names like these would read as a copy on sight.

## How did Graza become popular so fast?

Graza went live on its own website on January 11, 2022, [Marketing Brew reported in 2022](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2022/02/17/inside-the-social-strategy-that-squeezy-bottle-olive-oil-brand-graza-used-to-make-its-dtc-debut), and an audience was already waiting. A simple landing page had collected about 2,000 email addresses before launch, and starting about three weeks out, the team mailed roughly 25 packs of oil a week to food creators with no posting requirement, per a [2023 Klaviyo case study](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/guerrilla-marketing-for-ecommerce). Marketing Brew counted more than 100 packages sent before the site went live, with smaller creators getting oil weeks early and accounts with 200,000 or more followers getting it closer to launch day.

The list was picked for curiosity more than reach. Social media consultant Kendall Dickieson told Marketing Brew, "We went after people that if you saw this on their account, people would want to ask about it." The list included barbecue and pizza enthusiasts and high-level chefs, chosen to widen how people pictured using olive oil. Graza also offered samples and free staff bottles to about 80 retailers, including pet stores and kids' toy stores, so shop owners got the same early, no-strings treatment as creators. Bloom Nutrition grew from the opposite direction, with a founder who was already the creator, which I cover in [Bloom's community playbook](/blog/bloom-nutrition-marketing-strategy).

The payoff came in launch week. Cookbook author Molly Baz ran out of her gifted oil, Graza sent her a refill of 5-liter restaurant jugs, and she posted them. "The second she posted that on her story, it was a bloodbath," Benin told Klaviyo. Graza sold out in its first week with $100,000 in revenue and passed $500,000 by April, without paid ads, per the same case study. Marketing Brew reported a 7.91% site conversion rate from Graza's Instagram bio in the first seven days.

That means the early growth came from viewers spotting an unfamiliar bottle in a creator's kitchen and asking about it. That only happens if you put the product there first.

## Graza ran the grocery launch in reverse

The usual grocery path starts with slotting fees, payments to a retailer for shelf space before any shopper has tried the product. The [FTC's 2003 staff report on slotting allowances](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2003/11/ftc-releases-grocery-industry-slotting-allowance-report) found that slotting fees "can make up a large fraction of the revenues earned by some products in their first year."

Graza let the shelf come to it. After the online sellout, a Whole Foods representative reached out, and by the end of that year Graza's bottles were on Whole Foods shelves nationwide, [Inside Retail Asia reported in 2025](https://insideretail.asia/2025/12/16/how-graza-became-a-48-million-olive-oil-brand-stocked-by-whole-foods-and-target/). Target, Costco, and Walmart followed, and the same story estimated 2024 revenue at $48.4 million.

So the expensive steps came after Graza had sales to show for the cheap ones, which is the main thing a small launch budget can learn from it.

## What to take from Graza's marketing strategy

1. **Start from the objection.** Anthony's "for everyone" set Graza's price and its everyday tone at once, so find the complaint your product answers before you design anything.
2. **Design the on-camera moment first.** Decide what the product does in a 10-second video, then build the package around that action.
3. **Seed for curiosity.** Send product to creators and shops whose followers would ask about it, and attach no posting requirement.
4. **Prove demand online, then pitch retail.** A sold-out first week is a stronger pitch to a buyer than a forecast, and it can save you the cost of buying your way onto the shelf.
5. **Name the job.** A name that says what the product does is easy to choose from a shelf and can stretch to a new product line.

## The catch: the bottle stopped being Graza's alone

This is where I part ways with the usual read. Most of the top-ranking Graza breakdowns credit the bottle, but it is the one piece of the strategy that stopped being Graza's alone.

Competitors copied it first. California Olive Ranch launched a 500 ml squeezable "Chef's Bottle" in March 2024 after customers called asking for one, [Modern Retail reported in April 2024](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/to-squeeze-or-not-to-squeeze-the-olive-oil-world-is-divided-over-plastic-packaging/), and Benin had earlier accused Brightland of copying the design on LinkedIn before apologizing. The same story quoted Kosterina founder Katina Mountanos: "Olive oil should never be packaged in plastic."

Then Graza moved past the bottle itself. On August 7, 2025, it began selling Sizzle and Drizzle in glass, and Benin told [The Dieline](https://thedieline.com/ex-squeeze-me-did-graza-just-go-glass/) there had been "clear feedback from some people who simply prefer their Extra Virgin Olive Oil in glass." Costs pressed on the model too: [Modern Retail reported in October 2023](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/how-brands-like-graza-are-dealing-with-olive-oil-price-hikes/) that Graza was paying more than double its early 2022 import costs and had raised its price from $15 to $16 a bottle.

Image: Graza." ratio="6/7" />

At 28,000 locations, most shoppers meet Graza on a shelf near other squeeze bottles, so the name and label do more of the selling than the shape. That is why I think the 2026 mayo launch is the real test. If people buy Graza mayo, the brand is the names and the voice, and the bottle was only ever the hook. I'd also have offered glass as a choice in year two, before critics could frame it as a concession.

So a packaging hook lasts about as long as it takes a competitor to order molds, and Graza's names kept working after that. If you're launching something this year, test the hook and the name separately. Post the product with no logo in frame and see whether people ask what it is, then put the name on a plain package and see whether people know what it's for. Graza needed both, and only one of them got harder to copy with time.
