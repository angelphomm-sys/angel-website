"Why did you choose Utah Valley University (UVU)?"

I've been asked that question throughout college, and the honest answer is that someone told me to.

I came to Utah to start over. I had a dream for myself, and it didn't work out. After a lot of pivots, I felt lost and unsure of what to do next. My boyfriend suggested I go to college "to figure it out." Somehow he sold me on it. I took his advice, but he couldn't help much with the rest. His parents had done everything for him, so he didn't know the process either.

I figured out the application on my own. I figured out the Free Application for Federal Student Aid, the FAFSA, on my own. I figured out all the small things nobody tells you when you're the first in your family to do this.

But I think a better question is, "Why did you stay at UVU?"

## My answer is freedom

Not the freedom to do whatever I want, or the kind that comes with a better job. I mean the freedom to live without reservations. Here, I found the courage to be myself, to choose my own path, and to decide what actually matters to me. I stopped letting the world decide who I was supposed to be.

Before UVU, freedom was a concept I understood. Now I can see what it looks like.

## You can only picture the kind of life you've seen

I grew up in a household without much education. Looking back, I can see how limited my thinking was.

In high school, I made a slideshow about my future. It had my hometown, a career I was sure about, the people I looked up to, and where I saw myself in five years. I meant all of it.

Almost none of it happened that way.

For a while, I thought that meant I had failed. Like the plan was a promise, and changing it made me unreliable. I think a lot of people carry that quietly. We hold on to what we believed before we had lived much, as if growing up means protecting the opinions of someone who didn't have much information yet.

But I did believe it, and that's worth saying. It was real when I believed it.

## What changed is that I kept going

I left home. At UVU, I sat in rooms I had never been in before. As one of the first Hope Scholars, I spoke at donor events and board meetings to raise money for students like me. I proposed a marketing internship and then got to do it. I studied abroad in Italy. I learned about jobs I had never heard of, and I met people who showed me a kind of life I couldn't have described at eighteen because I had never seen one.

The plan didn't fall apart. I outgrew it.

There's a difference between contradicting yourself and evolving. Contradicting yourself is pretending you never said it. Evolving is being able to say, "I believed that once, and I don't anymore."

People don't always leave room for that. They get attached to the version of you they already understand. When you change, they say "you changed" like it's an accusation instead of saying you grew. Sometimes the person holding on to the old version is you.

## The old plan wasn't wrong. It was early.

If you look at what you wanted back then, the thing underneath it usually survives. For me, it was wanting to help people. That never moved. The career changed, the timeline changed, and the shape of it changed completely, but the reason stayed the same.

That isn't a broken promise. It's the same person with better information.

## It doesn't stop with me

That's what education gave me as a first-generation student. It showed me more options than I knew existed.

I'm the sister who is making the path easier for my siblings. Every form I had to figure out alone is one they won't have to. I want to keep doing that for them and for other students who come from where I come from.

I left home for my dreams, and I built a home away from home here.

So why did I stay at UVU? Because this is where I learned I'm allowed to grow. If you're further from your old plan than you expected, you didn't betray the person who made it. You just stopped treating their plan like a life sentence.

If you're the first in your family to do this, what made you stay where you are?
