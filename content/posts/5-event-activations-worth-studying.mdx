Most lists of brand activations are lists of budgets. A car company builds a maze. A streaming service rents a warehouse. You read it, you think that looks expensive, and you learn nothing you could use on Monday.

The activations worth studying are the ones where you can point at a single decision and say, that is the thing that worked. Sometimes the budget is a rented counter and a folding table.

Here are five, in order of what they taught me. Two of them I stood in.

## 01 · JJ Coffee opens in Salt Lake, and the line becomes the ad

In December 2025, a coffee shop from Orem opened a second location on 400 South in Salt Lake City. People lined up out the door on the first morning.

The interesting part is not the line. It is that JJ Coffee had spent the year before selling house-made syrups online, shipped anywhere, to people who had never been in the Orem shop. By the time the Salt Lake doors opened, there was already a group of people in the city who owned the product and had never had the drink.

The decision worth studying: they built the audience in the wrong order on purpose. Product first, at a distance. Then the room.

## What the five have in common

Three things showed up in every one of them.

**Someone had to hand something over.** A drink, a bag, a plate, a promo code. An activation without an exchange is a display.

**The moment had to be worth a photo without being about the photo.** The ones that beg to be posted get posted less.

**Somebody had to be counting.** The activations I would call successful all had a number attached before they started: bottles sold at the counter, redemptions at the gate, emails at the door. The ones that did not were fun and unrepeatable, which is another way of saying they cannot be funded again.

That last one is the difference between an event and a marketing activation. The event happens either way. The activation knows why.
